In Mongolia, 25.45% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Mongolia

The most surprising figure in Mongolia's data is the share of conversations that look like coursework or learning: 34.11%, more than double the global share of 16.45%. A reader assuming usage patterns simply track a country's wealth would not have guessed that. Education & Learning is also the single largest request topic at 25.45% of sampled conversations, against a global average of 13.23% — a 92.4% lead. This does not tell us who is holding these conversations; it tells us what the conversations contain, and a great many of them are structured around teaching, explanation, and study-shaped tasks. One plausible reading is that the tool is being used as an always-available tutor — something that fits naturally in a place where Mongolian, Korean, and English all circulate and where a patient, on-demand explainer has obvious value — but that is a guess, not a finding.

Mongolia's Anthropic Usage Index is 1.37, ranked 49 of 121, against Korea's 3.78. And yet the education comparison runs the other way: 25.45% of conversations versus Korea's 11.21%, so Mongolia's share on that topic is 127% higher. Hobbies & Lifestyle is another lead, 8.15% against Korea's 6.44%. Work-related conversations make up 32.84% in Mongolia compared with 43.36% globally, so the centre of gravity sits closer to learning than to office tasks.

Within work-matched conversations, though, the lean toward automation is clear: 57.44% automation versus 42.56% augmentation, while globally automation sits at 48.62%. Document Processing & Extraction accounts for 7.19% of topics, well above the global 4.32%, and conversations matched to office and administrative support tasks come in at 9.52% against a global 7.89%. Taken together, the picture is of people handing over discrete, document-heavy tasks — forms, text extraction, routine paperwork — rather than using the tool purely as a collaborator.

Conversations matched to software development tasks are at 8.94%, below the global 11.51%, while content creation and copywriting at 21.34% tracks the global 22.72% almost exactly. Software conversations being under the global share says nothing about the quality or quantity of people building software; it is a mix of what gets asked, and it suggests builders in Mongolia may rely on these tools more for the surrounding work than for the code itself.
