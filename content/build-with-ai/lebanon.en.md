## What the data says about Lebanon

Lebanon's Anthropic Usage Index sits at 0.96, ranking 61st out of 121 countries — far below Korea's 3.78. If you only looked at raw intensity, you might assume the character of use would be shallower too. The data says the opposite. A full quarter of conversations from Lebanon, 25.52%, look like coursework or learning, against a global share of 16.45%. And in the topic breakdown, Education & Learning takes 19.8% of sampled conversations, where the global figure is 13.23% and Korea's is 11.21%. On that measure Lebanon isn't behind Korea at all — it leads by 76.6%.

The lead extends to software work. Conversations about Software Development make up 14.77% of Lebanon's sample, versus 11.51% globally and 10.42% in Korea — a 41.7% edge over Korea. Conversations matched to Computer and Mathematical job tasks run at 27.49%, above the global 23.8%. Meanwhile Research & Intelligence sits at 8.22%, a quarter under the global 10.94%, and Sales-related tasks at 7.66%, below the global 9.14%. The shape that suggests is usage tilted toward building and studying rather than analysis or selling. That's a plausible reading, not a proven one: the figures describe conversations matched to tasks, not the people having them, and a conversation that looks like coursework doesn't tell us who typed it.

Work conversations are 43.89% of the total, essentially level with the global 43.36%, so the difference isn't that Lebanon uses these tools more casually. The difference is in the mix. On the automation-versus-augmentation axis, Lebanon leans toward augmentation — 54.48% against 45.52% automation, while globally automation edges ahead at 48.62%. People there appear somewhat more inclined to work alongside the model than to hand tasks over outright, which sits comfortably next to the heavy learning share.

None of this will surprise anyone who knows Lebanon's technical culture: multilingual, outward-facing, with Beirut's time zone bridging Europe, the Gulf, and an evening overlap with Seoul.
