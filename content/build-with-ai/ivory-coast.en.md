In Ivory Coast, 21.16% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Ivory Coast

Start with the comparison that breaks the expected ordering. Korea's Anthropic Usage Index is 3.78; Ivory Coast's is 0.3, putting it 102nd of 121 countries. Yet within the conversations that happen, Education & Learning claims 21.16% of Ivory Coast's sample — against 11.21% in Korea, an 88.8% higher share. Software Development runs 12.3% of conversations, 18% above Korea's 10.42%. Even Content Creation & Copywriting, the largest single topic at 30.84%, sits ahead of Korea's 27.3%. On overall volume the two countries are nowhere near each other; on what the conversations are actually about, Ivory Coast leads in all three of the biggest categories. If you assumed the richer index would dominate every dimension, this country's data says otherwise.

The learning signal shows up twice, which makes it harder to dismiss. Beyond the Education & Learning topic share — 21.16% against a global 13.23% — 27.57% of all conversations are classified as coursework or learning, versus 16.45% globally. That is the widest gap in this country's profile, roughly two-thirds above the world average. The data says nothing about who is having these conversations, and I won't speculate about that. What it does indicate is that an unusually large portion of the dialogue is structured like studying, practising, and being taught — and that this holds whether you measure by topic or by conversation type.

The rest of the profile reinforces a making-things character rather than a looking-things-up one. Research & Intelligence accounts for just 5.21% of conversations, barely half the global 10.94%. Meanwhile Document Processing & Extraction runs at 6.41% against 4.32% globally, and conversations matched to office and administrative support tasks reach 10.43% versus 7.89%. Sales-related task conversations are comparatively light at 6.49% against 9.14%. The automation-versus-augmentation balance tips 52.94% toward automation, a bit above the global 48.62%. My reading — hedged, because one snapshot can't prove intent — is of conversations oriented toward producing finished output: drafts, documents, code, exercises, with comparatively little open-ended research in the mix.

That orientation is exactly where my experience is most relevant.
