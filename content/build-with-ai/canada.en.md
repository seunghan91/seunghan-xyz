In Canada, 12.94% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about Canada

Canada's most surprising number is not a work number. Conversations matched to Hobbies & Lifestyle tasks account for 12.94% of the Canadian sample — 36.4% above the global average of 9.49%, and 100.9% higher than Korea's 6.44%. That is the single widest gap between Canada and Korea anywhere in this dataset, and it belongs to a country that hardly needs to prove itself: Canada posts an Anthropic Usage Index of 4.13, ranked 6 of 121, ahead of Korea's 3.78. Anyone assuming a simple ordering where usage intensity tracks down a ladder of economies has it backwards here — a top-six country is pouring an outsized share of its conversations into personal interests, side projects, and the parts of life that never appear on an invoice.

What that plausibly reflects is usage spread well beyond formal obligations. Work conversations are 42.11% of the Canadian sample, almost exactly the global 43.36%. Coursework and learning conversations, though, are just 8.25% — half the global 16.45% — and Education & Learning as a topic runs 28.6% below the global average. Meanwhile Research & Intelligence over-indexes at 13.21% against a global 10.94%. One honest reading of that combination, hedged as a guess: the conversations skew toward self-directed questions rather than assigned exercises. This is one snapshot with no trend line, so nothing here says where the pattern came from or where it heads next — only what it looks like now. The automation-versus-augmentation split, 47.33% to 52.67%, sits close to the global 48.62% automation mark, so Canadians delegate to the model and collaborate with it in roughly the same proportions as everyone else.

Inside the work share, Content Creation & Copywriting leads at 20.99%, slightly under the global 22.72%, and conversations matched to Sales and Related job tasks run 23.3% above the global average — a quiet pocket of intensity. Software Development sits at 9.23%, below the global 11.51%, which means the demand is anything but concentrated in engineering topics.

For someone in Canada already building, this is favourable ground: a large base of conversations already organised around things people chose to make.
