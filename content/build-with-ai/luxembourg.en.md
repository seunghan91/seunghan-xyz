## What the data says about Luxembourg

Fourth place. Out of 121 countries measured on the Anthropic Usage Index, Luxembourg sits at 4.85 — ahead of Korea's 3.78, and ahead of essentially everywhere else. Anyone expecting AI usage intensity to sort neatly by population or by some assumed hierarchy of tech nations will find that number awkward. A small country wedged between France, Belgium, and Germany, better known for banking than for software, is generating one of the densest streams of AI conversation on the planet relative to its size.

The character of those conversations is telling. Work accounts for 42.82%, a hair under the global 43.36%, while coursework-shaped conversations run at 12.18% — well under the global 16.45%. And the balance between handing tasks over to the tool versus working alongside it leans collaborative: 53.21% augmentation against 46.79% automation, a touch more partnership-oriented than the global split. Read together, and with the caveat that this is one snapshot, the pattern looks like AI treated as a working instrument by people with established tasks, not a novelty or a homework shortcut.

The sharpest deviation is negative. Conversations matched to software development tasks make up only 9.62% of the sample, versus 11.51% globally — the country's largest gap from the norm at -16.4%. What fills the space instead: content creation and copywriting at 20.2%, research and intelligence above the global share at 11.96%, and job-task matches for sales and related work running 15.3% hot at 10.54%. For a country whose economy revolves around finance, legal services, and cross-border business conducted in several languages, that tilt toward text, analysis, and persuasion over code is plausible — though the data can't say why, only that it is so.

Against Korea specifically, Luxembourg's clearest lead is personal, not professional. Hobbies and lifestyle conversations run 10.9% there against Korea's 6.44% — 69.3% higher — and education and learning edges ahead too, 11.68% to 11.21%. Whatever else the numbers mean, these tools are already woven into ordinary life in Luxembourg.
