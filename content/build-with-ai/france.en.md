## What the data says about France

France posts an Anthropic Usage Index of 3.97 — tenth out of 121 countries — which puts it ahead of Korea at 3.78, the country where this site's author writes his code. Anyone expecting usage intensity to stack neatly along a rich-country ladder has to reckon with that ordering. But the more revealing number sits underneath it: only 34.23% of conversations matched to job tasks in France are work conversations, against a global figure of 43.36%. Heavy usage, light on the day-job. That combination is France's signature in this data.

Where does the rest of the attention go? A good chunk lands on life outside the office. Hobbies and lifestyle conversations make up 12.99% of the French sample — 36.9% above the global 9.49%, and double Korea's 6.44%, a gap of 101.7%. Education and learning follows at 13.95%, ahead of both the global 13.23% and Korea's 11.21%. Conversations that looked like coursework account for 17.65%, a touch above the global 16.45%. One careful reading is that French users bring these tools into their evenings and sidelines: the personal project, the thing being learned for its own sake. The data describes conversations, not the people holding them, so it can't say who is behind the keyboard — only what the requests resembled.

The work-shaped slice has its own accent. Content creation and copywriting leads at 22.51%, essentially the global norm of 22.72%. Conversations matched to software development tasks sit at 9.16%, a fifth below the global 11.51%, while sales-related tasks run hot at 11% against 9.14% globally. And when France talks to a model, it prefers a collaborator over a delegate: augmentation leads automation 54.4% to 45.6%, a clearer tilt toward keeping a human in the loop than the global split shows. None of this is a trend — it is one snapshot — but it is a coherent one: a country of makers, writers, and tinkerers using AI as an instrument, not a substitute.
