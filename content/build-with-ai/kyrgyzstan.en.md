## What the data says about Kyrgyzstan

Kyrgyzstan's Anthropic Usage Index is 0.35, ranked 99 of 121 countries — barely a tenth of Korea's 3.78. A simple rich-country/poor-country ordering would predict a thin, utilitarian profile. The data refuses: 12.56% of sampled conversations from Kyrgyzstan were matched to software development tasks, ahead of both the global 11.51% and Korea's 10.42%. A country with a tenth of Korea's overall usage intensity devotes proportionally more of its AI traffic to code.

The strongest signal in the profile is learning-shaped traffic. Conversations matched to Education & Learning topics make up 17.23% of the sample, against a global 13.23% — the country's largest positive deviation at +30.2% — and 22.76% of conversations looked like coursework or learning, versus 16.45% globally. These figures describe conversations, not people; a conversation that resembles coursework reveals nothing about who typed it. The cautious reading is that the tools are being used heavily as instruments of explanation and practice, not only as vending machines for finished answers.

Work is comparatively scarce in the mix. Only 32.53% of conversations were work-related, far under the global 43.36%, and tasks matched to office and administrative support ran at 6.28% versus 7.89% globally. What occupies the space instead is personal: Hobbies & Lifestyle conversations accounted for 10.22%, 58.7% higher than Korea's 6.44%. Add the education gap — 17.23% against Korea's 11.21%, a 53.7% lead — and Kyrgyzstan's sample is, on all three of these measures, more learning-and-leisure weighted than the country where I live. Research & Intelligence is the notable underweight at 7.95% versus 10.94% globally, while content creation sits almost exactly at the global norm: 21.91% against 22.72%.

On style of use, the country sits near the world midpoint but tips directive: 50.95% of conversations leaned toward automation — handing the model a task — against 49.05% augmentation, with the global automation share at 48.62%. Combined with the low research share, the pattern suggests sessions with a concrete deliverable in mind rather than open-ended exploration.
