## What the data says about Slovenia

Slovenia's most distinctive number is not its overall usage index — 2.13, placing it 30th of 121 countries — but what fills those conversations. Tasks matched to software development account for 14.66% of sampled Slovenian conversations, against a global average of 11.51%. That is a 27.4% premium over the worldwide baseline, the single strongest deviation in the country's profile, and it cuts against any assumption that usage patterns simply track national wealth. Korea, with a far higher overall index of 3.78, logs only 10.42% of conversations on software development tasks. Slovenia exceeds that by 40.7%. On this measure, Slovenia is plainly ahead of Korea, and the data says so without qualification.

What might that reflect? A reasonable, hedged reading is that people turning to these tools in Slovenia bring an unusually technical set of problems to them. The pattern is consistent elsewhere in the numbers: content creation and copywriting, the most common task category globally at 22.72%, takes a smaller share in Slovenia at 19.91%. Less drafting, more building. The split between automation-leaning and augmentation-leaning conversations sits nearly even — 48.69% versus 51.31% — almost identical to the global 48.62% automation figure, so the technical tilt is not a story about handing work over wholesale. It looks more like people staying in the loop while delegating well-specified pieces.

The second place Slovenia leads Korea is quieter but telling: hobbies and lifestyle tasks make up 10.01% of conversations, 55.4% above Korea's 6.44%. Coursework-flavoured conversations are also slightly overrepresented — 18.19% versus a global 16.45% — which says only that the conversations resembled coursework, not who was having them. Read together, the picture is of usage that reaches past the office. Work-related conversations are 41.55% of the total, marginally below the global 43.36%, while research and intelligence tasks at 10.95% sit exactly on the worldwide 10.94% line. The profile is technical, curious, and not confined to business hours.

For a small Central European country whose afternoons overlap comfortably with Seoul's evenings, this is a profile I enjoy working with, because the conversations I have tend to start from code that already exists. If you are shipping something, I can help with the unglamorous parts that decide whether it survives: getting apps through store review, wiring up AI agents and MCP tooling so they hold up outside a demo, backend and authentication design, mobile development, and reliability engineering for when real users arrive.
