In Poland, 12.59% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about Poland

Poland's most distinctive number in this dataset is not about work at all. Conversations matched to hobbies and lifestyle tasks make up 12.59% of sampled usage — a third above the global 9.49%, and nearly double Korea's 6.44%. That 95.5% gap over Korea is the largest lead either country holds over the other anywhere in this data, and it cuts against any simple ordering where a higher-usage country dominates every category. Korea's overall index is 3.78; Poland's is 1.33, ranked 52nd of 121. Yet in this slice of life, Polish conversations lean personal far more heavily.

Work is present but not dominant: 41.77% of conversations matched to work tasks, just under the global 43.36%. The automation-versus-augmentation balance (47.85% against 52.15%) sits almost exactly on the global line of 48.62%, so the texture of how these tools are used — directing tasks versus collaborating on them — looks ordinary. What is not ordinary is where the volume goes.

Conversations matched to software development tasks account for 13.17% of Polish usage, above the global 11.51% and ahead of Korea's 10.42% — a second, concrete category where Poland leads. Research and intelligence follows at 12%, also above the global 10.94%. Content creation is still the largest single topic at 20.08%, though below the global 22.72%. Meanwhile, conversations that look like coursework or education run well under the global average: 12.31% against 16.45% for learning overall, and 9.98% against 13.23% for education tasks specifically. One plausible reading, hedged as a guess from a single snapshot, is that Polish usage skews toward making things — side projects, code, research — rather than study-shaped sessions. The other local signature: conversations matched to sales and related job tasks, at 10.71% versus a global 9.14%.

None of this describes who the users are; it describes what the conversations looked like. But as a profile, it is a builder's profile with an unusually strong personal-projects streak.
