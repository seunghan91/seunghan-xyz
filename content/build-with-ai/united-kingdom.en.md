## What the data says about United Kingdom

The assumption about a wealthy, tech-saturated country is that AI usage there would be about efficiency — delegating tasks, automating work, getting more done. The UK data points the other way. Automation-leaning conversations make up just 44.81% of the country's total, against 55.19% augmentation — and the global average for automation is 48.62%. People in the UK are measurably more inclined to keep themselves inside the loop, using these tools to think with rather than to hand work over to. Only 39.02% of conversations are work-related at all, compared with 43.36% globally, so the majority of what gets discussed sits outside the job entirely.

What dominates instead is content and curiosity. Content Creation & Copywriting leads at 21.51%, near the global 22.72%, but the next three categories all outrun their global averages: Education & Learning at 15.14% versus 13.23%, Research & Intelligence at 12.29% versus 10.94%, and Hobbies & Lifestyle at 11.12% versus 9.49%. Conversations that looked like coursework account for 17.5% of the total, above the global 16.45%, and conversations matched to "Educational Instruction and Library" job tasks run 19.3% above average. Meanwhile the technical end is quieter: conversations matched to software development tasks are 8.16% of the total, 29.1% below the global 11.51%, and "Computer and Mathematical" job-task conversations trail by 15.1%. One hedged reading of this snapshot is that the UK engagement pattern is broad and exploratory — strong on understanding and personal projects, lighter on raw code generation.

Against Korea, whose usage index is higher at 3.78 to the UK's 3.35, the UK still leads decisively in two places. Hobbies & Lifestyle conversations are 72.7% more common — 11.12% versus 6.44% — and Education & Learning runs 35.1% higher at 15.14% versus 11.21%. On the exploratory side of the ledger, the UK profile is the stronger one, and it is worth saying so without a caveat.
