In United Arab Emirates, 7.62% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about United Arab Emirates

The number that jumps out of the United Arab Emirates data is the work share: 54.63% of sampled conversations are work-related, against a global average of 43.36%. That is one of the more work-saturated profiles in this dataset, and it pairs with a coursework-and-learning share of just 9.09%, barely half the global 16.45%. Whatever assumptions you bring about how usage distributes across countries, this profile does not look like people experimenting or studying. It looks like people getting things produced. The topic mix backs that reading: content creation and copywriting leads at 24.82%, research and intelligence follows at 12.41%, and both sit above their global averages, while education and learning trails at 9.99% against a global 13.23% — the single most distinctive gap in the country's profile, about 24.5% below the norm.

The automation-versus-augmentation split leans slightly toward automation, 50.28% to 49.72%, a touch above the global automation share. Read together with the work skew, it plausibly reflects conversations where the goal is a finished deliverable — a document, an analysis, a piece of code — rather than a back-and-forth that builds understanding. That is a guess about intent, but it is the most natural reading of the pattern.

Korea is a useful reference point because it sits higher on the overall usage index, at 3.78 against the UAE's 2.84. Yet the UAE matches or passes Korea in two categories. Conversations matched to software development tasks make up 10.48% of the UAE sample versus Korea's 10.42%, essentially level. And conversations matched to hobbies and lifestyle tasks are 7.62% against Korea's 6.44%, roughly 18% higher. On the task side, office and administrative support tasks stand out at 9.08% versus a global 7.89%, about 15% above the norm — another signal of office-shaped, output-shaped usage.

For someone building in the UAE, the practical takeaway is that the surrounding conversation is already heavily work-oriented, so the bottleneck is rarely finding ideas and more often shipping them well. That is exactly where Seunghan's free, informal help is aimed: getting apps through App Store and Play Store review, wiring up AI agents and MCP tooling, backend and authentication work, mobile development, and the reliability engineering that keeps a service alive after launch. Working days in the Gulf overlap usefully with evenings in Seoul, so a question sent after lunch tends to get an answer the same day.
