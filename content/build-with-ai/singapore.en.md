## What the data says about Singapore

The number that should reorder your assumptions is 5.81. That is Singapore's Anthropic Usage Index, good for rank 2 out of 121 countries — and comfortably ahead of Korea's 3.78, which is itself a heavy-usage country. If you expected AI usage to sort neatly along some simple rich/poor axis, Singapore's position near the very top of the table, as a small island state, is the surprise. The texture of the usage matters as much as the volume: 46.75% of sampled Singapore conversations are work-related, above the global share of 43.36%, while coursework-style conversations run just 10.97% against a global 16.45%.

What does a high-intensity, work-weighted profile actually contain? Mostly research. Conversations matched to Research & Intelligence tasks take 14.71% of the Singapore sample versus 10.94% globally — a 34.5% premium, the country's single most distinctive gap. Conversations matched to Business and Financial Operations tasks are similarly overrepresented at 7.65% versus 5.77%. And the flip side is just as telling: conversations matched to software development tasks come in at 9.05%, well under the global 11.51%. A reasonable guess, and only a guess, is that a compact economy dense with regional headquarters and finance functions pushes usage toward analysis and synthesis rather than code. The figures describe conversations, not people, so that is as far as the data goes.

Singapore also leans toward augmentation more than most: 53.7% of conversations versus 46.3% automation-leaning, where the global automation share is higher at 48.62%. Content creation is still the largest single topic at 20.56%. Against Korea specifically, Singapore leads on three fronts — Hobbies & Lifestyle (8.29% versus 6.44%), Research & Intelligence (14.71% versus 13.46%), and Education & Learning (11.74% versus 11.21%).
