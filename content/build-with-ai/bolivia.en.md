## What the data says about Bolivia

The number that breaks the usual assumptions is this one: 14.69% of sampled AI conversations in Bolivia are matched to software development tasks, compared to 10.42% in Korea. Bolivia's overall usage index is 0.56 — rank 81 of 121 — while Korea sits at 3.78, one of the most saturated usage profiles in the data. Yet on the share of conversations that look like building software, Bolivia runs 41% ahead. Lower volume, higher concentration of people making things.

The dominant texture of Bolivian usage, though, is learning. 43.26% of conversations look like coursework or learning, against a global average of 16.45%, and Education & Learning leads the topic list at 25.69% — nearly double the global 13.23% and 129% above Korea's 11.21%. A word of caution the data itself demands: these figures describe conversations, not the people in them. A conversation that looks like coursework doesn't tell you who typed it. What it does tell you is that the model is being used heavily as a teacher — explanations, walkthroughs, worked problems — rather than purely as a production tool.

There's a practical streak running alongside that. Document Processing & Extraction takes 7.61% of conversations, 76% above the global share, and conversations matched to Office and Administrative Support tasks run 31% above average while Sales-related ones run 33% below. One plausible reading is paperwork-shaped problems — extracting, reformatting, summarising documents — being handed to AI, but that's a guess from task categories, nothing more. Research & Intelligence, meanwhile, sits at about half the global share at 5.57%.

Work conversations account for 33.71% of the total versus 43.36% globally, and within them Bolivia tilts slightly toward automation — 51.56% against 48.44% augmentation, just above the global 48.62%. People there are marginally more likely to hand a task over whole than to iterate on it together with the model.

Content Creation & Copywriting rounds out the picture at 24.3%, close to the global 22.72% — the one place Bolivia looks almost exactly like everyone else.

Put together: a usage profile weighted toward learning, building, and documents, with software conversations punching well above what the raw index would predict.
