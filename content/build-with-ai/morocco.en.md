In Morocco, 21.48% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Morocco

Morocco's single most striking number is not its overall usage index — it sits at 0.9, 66th of 121 countries — but the shape of what fills those conversations. A full 34.89% of Moroccan conversations look like coursework or learning tasks, against a global average of 16.45%. That is more than double the worldwide share, and it mirrors the topic breakdown, where Education & Learning runs at 21.48% versus 13.23% globally, a 62.4% overrepresentation. Something in Morocco is pulling these tools hard toward teaching and being taught, at a scale the worldwide pattern does not predict.

Here is the part that should surprise anyone assuming a simple rich-country ordering: Morocco leads Korea in two categories. Education & Learning conversations are 91.6% more common than in Korea (21.48% versus 11.21%), and conversations matched to software development tasks are 35.6% more common (14.13% versus 10.42%). Korea's overall index is 3.78 — far higher — yet Moroccan conversations tilt more heavily toward code and coursework. The data cannot say who is on the other end of these conversations; a high coursework share means the conversations looked like coursework, nothing more. And the automation-versus-augmentation split, 47.71% against 52.29%, sits almost exactly on the global line of 48.62%, so these are conversations where the human stays in the loop rather than handing the task over wholesale.

The other distinctive tilt is Document Processing & Extraction at 6.12%, 41.7% above the global average — plausibly a reflection of a multilingual environment where Arabic, French, and English documents constantly need parsing, translating, and restructuring, though that is a guess from the data, not a fact in it. Research & Intelligence runs 28.8% below the global share, and Content Creation & Copywriting leads all topics at 24.86%. Conversations matched to educational instruction and library tasks also run high, 15.61% versus 12.79% globally.

If you are building in Morocco, the practical reading is this: the conversations here already center on learning, writing, and software — exactly the loop where engineering help compounds fastest.
