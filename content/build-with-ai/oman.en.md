In Oman, 19.07% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Oman

The number that breaks the easy ordering is 70.1%. Korea's Anthropic Usage Index stands at 3.78; Oman's is 0.73, placing it 69th of 121 countries. On overall adoption intensity, that is a wide gap. Yet when you look at what conversations from Oman are actually about, Education & Learning takes 19.07% of the sample — against Korea's 11.21%. Seven-tenths higher, from the country with one-fifth the usage index. If you arrived assuming the data would line up neatly along a rich-country axis, that figure asks you to revise.

The learning signal runs through the whole profile. Conversations matched to coursework or learning tasks make up 25.03% of Oman's traffic, versus a global average of 16.45%. Education & Learning as a topic sits at 19.07% against 13.23% worldwide — the single largest deviation from the global average anywhere in Oman's data, 44% above baseline. A careful reading is that these tools are being used heavily for structured, study-shaped exchanges: explanations, worked problems, material that resembles coursework. I stress "resembles" deliberately — the figures describe conversations matched to task categories, not the people holding them, so nothing here says who those users are.

Oman also leads Korea where the work gets technical. Conversations matched to software development tasks account for 12.23% of the sample versus Korea's 10.42%, and the automation split is nearly balanced — 50.08% of conversations lean toward handing the task over, 49.92% toward collaborating with the model, both close to the global automation figure of 48.62%. Work-shaped conversations sit at 43.16%, essentially level with the global 43.36%. Content Creation & Copywriting leads the topic list at 23.48%, just above the worldwide 22.72%.

The quieter side of the profile is worth noting too. Hobbies & Lifestyle, at 6.91%, leads Korea's 6.44% yet runs 27% below the global 9.49% — one of Oman's most distinctive deviations. Research & Intelligence comes in at 9.04% against 10.94% globally. Conversations matched to Office and Administrative Support tasks run high at 9.7% versus 7.89% worldwide, while Sales and Related tasks run low at 7.27% versus 9.14%. Taken together, the pattern leans toward learning plus concrete production, and away from exploration and leisure.

That combination — study-shaped conversations alongside real software and content output — maps closely to how I can actually be useful. I help people ship: apps onto the iOS and Play stores, AI agents and MCP integrations, backend systems and auth, mobile development, and the reliability work that keeps a live service alive. The help is free and informal, and the Seoul–Muscat time difference leaves a generous overlap in our working days. If you are mid-build and want a second engineer on it, that is the conversation I am here for.
