## What the data says about Portugal

Portugal's Anthropic Usage Index stands at 3.32, seventeenth out of 121 countries — within reach of Korea's 3.78, a figure often cited as a benchmark for heavy everyday AI use. Anyone expecting the rankings to mirror a simple rich-country hierarchy would not predict that gap being so narrow. The index measures intensity of use against what a country's economic profile would suggest, and Portugal's score says conversations there are sustained and purposeful: 43.8% of them are work, essentially matching the global 43.36%.

The working style tilts toward collaboration. In 52.51% of conversations the model is a partner rather than a substitute, with automation-leaning requests at 47.49% — a shade more augmentation-heavy than the global picture, where automation reaches 48.62%. That pattern typically shows up as iteration: refining drafts, debugging code, sharpening analysis, rather than handing over whole tasks untouched.

Portugal also leads Korea outright in two categories. Conversations matched to hobbies and lifestyle tasks make up 9.03% of the sample versus Korea's 6.44% — a 40.2% gap, the largest in the data. And conversations matched to software development tasks sit at 11.71% against Korea's 10.42%, 12.4% higher. That second lead deserves a plain statement: software development is already Portugal's second-largest use case, above the global 11.51%, and it still runs ahead of a country known for engineering intensity. On these two measures, Portugal is simply in front.

The sharpest deviation from the global average is Education and Learning at 11.08%, well under the global 13.23%. Yet conversations matched to coursework or learning tasks run at 17.91%, above the global 16.45%. One plausible reading — a guess, since the data shows conversation shapes rather than people — is that learning arrives folded into doing: inside building, writing, and research sessions rather than as its own subject. Content creation and copywriting leads everything at 24.35%, above the global 22.72%, with research and intelligence at 11.07%, nearly level with software development.
