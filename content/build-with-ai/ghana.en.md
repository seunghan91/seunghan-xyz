## What the data says about Ghana

The number that should rearrange your assumptions is 29.57%. That is the share of conversations from Ghana matched to coursework or learning tasks, against a global figure of 16.45% — nearly double. Someone expecting a simple rich-country/poor-country ordering might assume that lower overall usage (Ghana's Anthropic Usage Index is 0.38, ranked 95 of 121) means shallower use. Instead, the single largest structural feature of Ghana's snapshot is that conversations there leaned far more toward learning than the world average, while work conversations came in at 37.78%, below the global 43.36%.

That learning tilt shows up everywhere you look. Education & Learning as a request topic hit 23.4%, versus 13.23% globally — a 76.9% gap, the widest divergence in the data — and conversations matched to educational instruction and library job tasks ran 27.8% above the global share. A plausible reading, offered as a guess: people are using the tool as something between a tutor and a study partner, working through material in dialogue. The data cannot say who sits on the other side of those conversations, only what they resembled.

And here the expected ordering inverts entirely. Ghana leads Korea — my home country, usage index 3.78 — in three categories. Conversations matched to software development tasks were 13.06% of Ghana's sample against Korea's 10.42%, a 25.3% lead. Education & Learning: 23.4% to Korea's 11.21%, more than double. Content Creation & Copywriting: 28.59% to 27.3%. Document Processing & Extraction also ran 31.5% above the global share, while Research & Intelligence sat 37% below it and sales-related tasks 27.4% below. The shape of use in Ghana skews toward producing things — writing, code, processed documents — rather than researching them.

One more detail worth noting: 54.68% of Ghana's conversations leaned toward automation, handing the whole task to the model, versus 45.32% augmentation. The global split is closer to even at 48.62%. Delegating entire tasks is precisely where software gets fragile in interesting ways.
