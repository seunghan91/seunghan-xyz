In Uganda, 26.56% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Uganda

The most striking figure in Uganda's data is not how little the tools are used but what the usage is for. Yes, the Anthropic Usage Index sits at 0.11, rank 116 of 121, against Korea's 3.78 — the volume is thin. But 26.56% of sampled conversations are education and learning, double the global 13.23% and 136.9% above Korea's 11.21%. One conversation in three reads as coursework, versus 16.45% globally, and 17.23% of conversations match educational instruction and library job tasks, a third over the global rate. However modest the count, the intent is concentrated.

And here is the number that inverts the assumed ordering: 14.34% of conversations matched to software development tasks, against Korea's 10.42%. Uganda leads the country I work in by 37.6% on this measure. Around it sits a coherent cluster — 26.49% on content creation and copywriting, 6.46% on document processing and extraction (+49.5% over global), 9.93% matching office and administrative support tasks. What the conversations are not is exploratory: research and intelligence runs at 6.16%, well below the global 10.94%. The pattern is production, not browsing — code, copy, documents, finished things.

The automation split reinforces that reading. 56.02% of conversations lean automation — hand the whole task over — against 48.62% globally, with augmentation at 43.98%. Meanwhile 42.06% of conversations are work-related, essentially level with the global 43.36%. A guess, hedged as one: where usage is thinner, each session carries a deliverable. Nobody is idly co-piloting; the ask is "finish this." That is precisely the point where generated output needs an engineer's pass — the draft is fast, and the review, the data model, and the deploy pipeline are what make it real.
