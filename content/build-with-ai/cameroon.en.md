In Cameroon, 27.53% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Cameroon

A usage index of 0.38 puts Cameroon 95th out of 121 countries, but the composition of that usage tells a different story from the ranking. Look at what the conversations are actually about: software development accounts for 12.3% of the Cameroon sample, above both the global 11.51% and Korea's 10.42%. A country whose overall index is one-tenth of Korea's 3.78 devotes a larger slice of its conversations to building software. Lower volume, sharper focus.

The strongest single signal in the data is educational. Conversations that looked like coursework or learning make up 41.1% of the Cameroon sample — the global figure is 16.45%. Education and learning as a request topic sits at 27.53%, more than double the global 13.23% and 145.6% above Korea's 11.21%, while conversations matched to educational instruction and library tasks run 18.28% against a global 12.79%. Meanwhile work conversations come in at 32.4%, well under the global 43.36%. That combination is worth reading carefully: it describes what the conversations resembled, not who was having them. A plausible reading, offered as a guess, is that the tools are being used heavily as instruments of study and explanation — less as a workplace appliance, more as something closer to a library with a tutor inside it.

What people ask for reinforces the making-things tilt. Content creation and copywriting is the single largest topic at 30.06%, ahead of the global 22.72% and Korea's 27.3%. Document processing and extraction runs hot at 5.99% versus 4.32% globally. Research and intelligence, by contrast, is half the global rate — 5.69% against 10.94%. And on the automation-versus-augmentation axis, the Cameroon sample leans toward delegation: 55.19% automation against 44.81% augmentation, while the global split is 48.62% the other way. Taken together, the picture is of conversations aimed at producing finished output — drafts, documents, code — rather than open-ended exploration.
