## What the data says about Bulgaria

Bulgaria's Anthropic Usage Index is 1.36, placing it 51st of 121 countries — a middling figure next to Korea's 3.78. Yet inside that modest volume sits a number that breaks the usual ordering: 14.88% of sampled conversations are matched to software development tasks, against a global average of 11.51% and Korea's 10.42%. That is 29.3% above the world and 42.8% above Korea. Lower overall usage, more technical content per conversation — the opposite of what a simple rich-country/poor-country ranking would predict.

The rest of the profile is consistent with that reading. Coursework and learning conversations account for only 10.66%, far under the global 16.45%, and education topics sit at 10.17% against a global 13.23%. Meanwhile 46.14% of conversations are work-related, above the global 43.36%. What people bring to these tools in Bulgaria skews toward doing rather than studying. To be precise about what this means: these figures describe what the conversations looked like, not who was having them. But a sample this tilted toward applied tasks, and this light on coursework, plausibly reflects a user base already past the exploratory stage and deep into real projects.

There is a second place where Bulgaria plainly leads Korea, and it is the more charming one. Hobbies and lifestyle topics make up 10.65% of Bulgarian conversations versus Korea's 6.44% — 65.4% higher, and above the global 9.49% too. Research and intelligence work also runs hot at 12.51% against a global 10.94%. The picture is of a tool being used for building things at work and for curiosity after hours. Conversations matched to office and administrative support tasks run 18.9% below the global average, while sales-related tasks run 18.8% above it, and the automation-versus-augmentation split — 48.61% to 51.39% — sits almost exactly on the global norm. Nothing unusual in how the tool is driven; plenty unusual in where it is aimed.

That combination — code-heavy, project-driven, hands-on — maps closely onto what Seunghan can actually help with. The sticking points in this kind of work are specific: pushing an app through store review, structuring AI agent and MCP tooling so the scaffolding does not eat the project, backend and auth decisions that are expensive to reverse, mobile development trade-offs, and the reliability engineering that turns a working demo into something people can depend on. Seoul runs six or seven hours ahead of Sofia, so a question sent late in your day tends to have an answer waiting by morning.
