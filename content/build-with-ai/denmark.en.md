## What the data says about Denmark

The single most surprising number in Denmark's data is how little of it looks like work. Only 35.36% of sampled conversations are work-related, against a global average of 43.36% — and this from a country whose Anthropic Usage Index of 3.57 puts it 15th of 121 countries, nearly level with Korea's 3.78. High usage, low work share. The assumption that heavy AI adoption in a wealthy economy means heavy office delegation does not hold here.

What fills the gap is learning. One in four Danish conversations — 25.31% — resembles coursework or learning material, compared with 16.45% globally. The automation-versus-augmentation split reinforces the pattern: Danish conversations lean toward augmentation 57.73% of the time, well above the global automation share of 48.62% on the other side. Read together, and hedged appropriately since these are conversation patterns rather than portraits of individual users, the data suggests people in Denmark reach for these tools as collaborators in figuring things out, not as machinery for clearing a task queue.

Denmark also leads Korea outright in two categories, and it is worth saying plainly. Conversations matched to Hobbies & Lifestyle tasks account for 11.11% of Denmark's sample versus Korea's 6.44% — 72.5% higher. Education & Learning conversations run 14.97% against Korea's 11.21%. The creative tilt shows up in job-task matching too: conversations matched to Arts, Design, Entertainment, Sports, and Media tasks run 16.2% above the global average, and those matched to Educational Instruction and Library tasks run 15.6% above it. The mirror image is software development: at 9.74%, conversations matched to software development tasks sit 15.4% below the global share, the largest negative gap in Denmark's profile.
