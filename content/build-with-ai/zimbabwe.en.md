In Zimbabwe, 28.7% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Zimbabwe

On the measure that ranks countries — the Anthropic Usage Index — Zimbabwe sits at 0.2, 111th of 121. On the measure that describes what people actually do with the tool, Zimbabwe is ahead of Korea in two categories. Conversations matched to software development tasks are 11.86% of Zimbabwe's sample against Korea's 10.42%, and education conversations are 28.7% against Korea's 11.21%. A simple rich-country/poor-country ordering would predict the opposite. The data does not cooperate with it.

The education figure is the axis everything else turns on. Globally, 13.23% of conversations concern Education and Learning; Zimbabwe more than doubles that. Coursework-shaped conversations are 39.57% of the total, where the global share is 16.45%. Precision matters here: the dataset classifies conversations, not people. A conversation that resembles coursework tells you the exchange looked like structured learning — nothing about who typed it or what they do for a living. The hedged reading is that a large fraction of Zimbabwean use treats the model as an instructor: explain this, walk me through it, check my work. Conversations matched to "Educational Instruction and Library" job tasks run at 20.25% against a global 12.79%, which is the same signal measured a different way.

The working style sharpens the picture further. Automation-leaning conversations are 57.63% versus 42.37% augmentation, while the world splits nearly even at 48.62%. Document Processing and Extraction runs at 7.63% against a global 4.32%. And the mirror image: Research and Intelligence, at 5.05%, is barely half the global 10.94%. Taken together, the shape is a user base with concrete objectives — hand over a defined task, receive a finished deliverable, learn the underlying material — and comparatively little appetite for open-ended wandering. Content creation at 27.11% fills much of the space between. Work conversations overall are 38.14%, below the global 43.36%, with the learning share accounting for the gap.

This is a snapshot, not a trajectory, so I won't claim anything about where it is heading. What it shows now is a conversation mix that is unusually technical and unusually purposeful for a country ranked 111th by volume.
