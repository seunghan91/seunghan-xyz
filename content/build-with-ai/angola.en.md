In Angola, 19.31% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Angola

A usage index of 0.1 — rank 118 of 121 — sounds like a footnote until you look at what fills the conversations. Angola's share of education and learning tasks is 19.31%, and Korea's is 11.21%. That is Angola ahead of Korea by 72.3%, not a rounding gap. Software development tasks: 15.08% against Korea's 10.42%. Content creation: 31.47% against 27.3%. On all three of Angola's biggest categories, the country with one-thirty-seventh of Korea's usage index runs a higher share. Whoever assumed the ordering would be simple was wrong.

The texture of that usage is unusually study-shaped. Conversations matched to coursework make up 28.51% of Angola's sample, against a global 16.45% — the largest gap in the whole dataset. That figure says nothing about who is typing; it only says the material brought to the model looks like learning far more often than almost anywhere else. Read alongside the education and software development shares, a plausible reading is that the model is being treated as a combined tutor, reference shelf, and workshop — one place where understanding the thing and producing the thing happen together.

The rest of the profile is just as specific. Document processing and extraction hits 6.51%, half again the global 4.32% — Angola's single most distinctive skew, hinting at real paperwork being converted into usable data. Research and intelligence sits at 5.65%, nearly half the global 10.94%: less browsing the world's questions, more finishing the task at hand. The direction of requests confirms it — 54.54% of conversations lean automation, delegating whole tasks, versus 45.46% augmentation, well above the global 48.62% automation split. Management-tagged tasks run 7.72% against a global 5.9%. Less work-shaped conversation overall (37.46% versus the global 43.36%), but what is there is pointed.
