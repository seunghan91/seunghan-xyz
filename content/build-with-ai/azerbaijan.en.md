In Azerbaijan, 17.04% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Azerbaijan

Azerbaijan's overall usage index is 0.43, placing it 90th of 121 countries measured, while Korea sits at 3.78 — nearly nine times the intensity. And yet on three of the five major conversation topics, Azerbaijan's sample leads Korea's. Conversations matched to education and learning tasks make up 17.04% of the Azerbaijani sample against Korea's 11.21%, a relative difference of about 52%. Software development tasks run 15.56% against 10.42%, roughly 49% higher. Hobbies and lifestyle edges ahead too, 9.48% to 6.44%. A simple ranking by national wealth or adoption volume would never predict that ordering, and it is the single most interesting thing in this country's data.

What does that plausibly reflect? Hedged, because the data only shows conversations matched to task categories, not the people having them: when usage volume is lower, the conversations that do happen appear to concentrate on learning and building rather than on routine office output. Conversations matched to office and administrative support tasks account for just 5.67% of Azerbaijan's sample, about 28% below the global 7.89%, while educational instruction and library tasks run 14.85%, around 16% above the global 12.79%. The work share overall is 36.25%, well under the global 43.36%, and coursework-like conversations are 18.88% against a global 16.45%. The automation-versus-augmentation split, meanwhile, is almost exactly the global norm — 48.58% against 51.42% — so nothing here suggests a distinctive reliance on handing tasks over wholesale.

The topic ranking tells the same story from another angle. Content creation and copywriting leads at 19.07%, but that is about 16% below the global 22.72%. Software development at 15.56% sits about 35% above the global 11.51%, and education and learning at 17.04% is roughly 29% above the global 13.23%. Hobbies and lifestyle, at 9.48%, matches the global 9.49% almost to the decimal. Research and intelligence trails slightly at 9.31% versus 10.94%. Taken together, the sample from Azerbaijan tilts away from producing text for an audience and toward acquiring skills and writing code — a profile closer to a workshop than a marketing department.

None of this says who the users are, and a single snapshot cannot say where the numbers are heading. But as a picture of what gets asked from a country straddling Europe and Asia, five hours behind Seoul, it is concrete: code and study dominate. That is precisely the territory where I can be useful, free and informally — shipping apps to the App Store and Google Play, building AI agents and MCP tooling, backend and auth work, mobile development, and the reliability engineering that keeps a project standing after launch. If the data describes the kind of thing you are already doing, the stuck parts are exactly what to bring me.
