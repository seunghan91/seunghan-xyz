In Hungary, 12.69% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about Hungary

Hungary's Anthropic Usage Index is 1.07, rank 56 out of 121 countries — roughly a third of Korea's 3.78. Look past that headline, though, and the picture flips. When you compare what conversations are actually *about*, Hungary leads Korea in three categories at once: conversations matched to software development tasks make up 13.57% of the Hungarian sample versus Korea's 10.42%, education and learning tasks 13.48% versus 11.21%, and hobbies and lifestyle tasks 12.69% versus just 6.44%. On that last one, Hungarian conversations run 97% higher than Korean ones. Lower overall volume, richer and more varied mix.

The hobbies number is the most distinctive thing in the entire dataset. At 12.69%, it sits 33.7% above the global average of 9.49% — the largest positive gap Hungary has against the world. A guess, and it is a guess: conversations matched to hobby and lifestyle tasks suggest people here are using these tools for things they chose to build, not only things they were assigned. Games, personal projects, tinkering that never had to justify itself to anyone. That reading fits with the fact that only 37.87% of conversations match work tasks, well under the global 43.36%.

Meanwhile, content creation and copywriting — the single biggest category worldwide at 22.72% — accounts for just 17.56% here, 22.7% below the global share. The skew runs toward making things rather than marketing them. Conversations that look like coursework or learning sit at 17.02%, roughly in line with the global 16.45%, and the split between automation-leaning and augmentation-leaning conversations (48.1% versus 51.9%) nearly mirrors the global balance. So there is nothing unusual about *how* the tools are being driven — only what they are pointed at.

That profile — AI used less for producing copy and more for building software, learning, and personal projects, with a task mix that outpaces Korea's despite a much smaller usage footprint — is a genuinely interesting place to be building from.
