In Tunisia, 28.16% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Tunisia

The number that jumps out of Tunisia's data is 51.52%: more than half of sampled Claude conversations matched to Tunisian usage look like coursework or learning tasks, against a global average of 16.45%. Only 27.94% of conversations look like work, where the global figure is 43.36%. If you assumed usage patterns simply track national income, this profile would not be what you predicted — and the surprise compounds when you look at what the learning-heavy conversations are actually about.

Education & Learning topics account for 28.16% of conversations, more than double the global 13.23%, and job tasks in Educational Instruction and Library run 16.96% against a global 12.79%. But here is the part worth sitting with: conversations matched to software development tasks make up 18.19%, well above the global 11.51%. A plausible reading — and it is a reading, not a fact — is that a lot of this coursework-looking usage is people teaching themselves to build software, or learning by building. The line between studying and shipping is blurry, and Tunisia's numbers sit right on that blur.

The comparison with Korea makes this concrete. Korea's overall usage index is higher at 3.78 against Tunisia's 1.4 (rank 48 of 121), but raw volume and topic mix are different things. On Education & Learning, Tunisia's 28.16% share is 151.2% higher than Korea's 11.21%, and on Software Development, Tunisia's 18.19% runs 74.6% ahead of Korea's 10.42%. On the topics closest to actually making software, conversations from Tunisia lean harder in that direction than conversations from Seoul.

The automation-versus-augmentation split is nearly even — 49.79% automation-leaning against 50.21% augmentation — slightly above the global automation share of 48.62%, which suggests people hand tasks to the model about as often as they collaborate with it. Content Creation & Copywriting sits at 22.49%, essentially at the global 22.72%, while Research & Intelligence is notably lower at 4.92% against a global 10.94%, and sales-related job tasks run 5.9% against a global 9.14%. Document Processing & Extraction at 5.96% edges above the global 4.32% — a small but real signal of practical, pipeline-style work alongside the learning.
