## What the data says about South Korea

The most counterintuitive number in South Korea's data is not how much people use these tools — it's how they use them. The country sits at 3.78 on the Anthropic Usage Index, rank 14 out of 121, which is heavy usage by any measure. Yet only 43.76% of Korean conversations lean toward automation, where the model carries out the task end to end, against 56.24% that lean toward augmentation, where the human keeps steering. The global automation figure runs the other way, at 48.62%. A simple rich-country/poor-country ordering would predict that heavier usage means more delegation; here, heavier usage comes with more collaboration. One plausible reading — and it is a reading, not a fact in the data — is that people in Korea tend to arrive with a clear sense of the output they want and treat the model as a working partner rather than a substitute.

The intent behind those conversations is equally telling. 47.24% are work-related, above the global 43.36%, while coursework and learning come in at 14.9%, below the global 16.45%. This is a snapshot, not a trend, but in this sample the centre of gravity is production, not practice.

What the work consists of is where Korea's signature really shows. Content Creation & Copywriting accounts for 27.3% of conversations, about a fifth above the global 22.72%, and Research & Intelligence runs at 13.46% versus 10.94% globally, roughly 23% higher. Conversations matched to tasks in arts, design, entertainment, sports, and media occupations reach 16.76%, nearly 24% above the global 13.55%, and business and financial operations tasks sit 22% above average. Hobbies & Lifestyle is the mirror image: 6.44% against a global 9.49%, about a third below. Even conversations matched to software development tasks, at 10.42%, come in slightly under the global 11.51%. The shape is consistent: writing, research, media, and commercial work, with little idle tinkering.
