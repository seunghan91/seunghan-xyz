In Trinidad and Tobago, 9.37% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about Trinidad and Tobago

Start with the number that cuts against the usual ranking story: in Trinidad and Tobago, 51.9% of sampled conversations lean toward automation — people handing tasks to the model outright — versus 48.1% treating it as a collaborator. The global automation share is 48.62%. At the same time, Research & Intelligence conversations run at just 8.24%, well below the global 10.94%, and conversations matched to sales tasks sit at 7.52% against 9.14% worldwide. The plausible reading, and it is only a reading, is a distinctly practical posture: these tools are being used to get things made, not to deliberate. Anyone assuming richer countries use AI more decisively would find the opposite pattern here — a twin-island energy economy out-delegating the global average.

What gets made is clear enough. Content Creation & Copywriting leads all topics at 24.41% of conversations, above the global 22.72%. Education & Learning follows at 15.96% — the country's largest deviation from the global baseline, 20.6% above the worldwide 13.23% — with coursework-style conversations at 15.85% overall. That describes the texture of the conversations, not the people having them; the data says what the chats looked like, never who was typing. Conversations matched to software development tasks account for 11.23%, essentially level with the global 11.51%, and 44.66% of all conversations are work-related, slightly above the global 43.36%.

The Korea comparison deserves plain statement. Korea's Anthropic Usage Index is 3.78 — far above Trinidad and Tobago's 1.05, which ranks 57th of 121 countries. Yet on the composition of usage, Trinidad and Tobago leads Korea in three categories: Education & Learning by 42.4%, Hobbies & Lifestyle by 45.5% (9.37% versus 6.44%), and conversations matched to software development tasks, 11.23% versus 10.42%. Higher intensity in Seoul, broader building-and-learning mix in Port of Spain and beyond. Neither fact diminishes the other, and only one of them fits the lazy rich-country/poor-country ordering.
