In Namibia, 28.42% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Namibia

Korea uses AI heavily — its Anthropic Usage Index is 3.78 — yet 28.42% of Namibia's sampled conversations concern Education & Learning, against just 11.21% of Korea's. Namibia leads Korea in that category by 153.5%. Its overall index is 0.44, ranked 88 of 121, but anyone reading that rank as a simple pecking order would miss where Namibia actually sits ahead. The learning tilt runs through everything: 36.38% of conversations look like coursework, more than double the global 16.45%, and conversations matched to Educational Instruction and Library tasks run 29.6% above the global share. To be careful about what this means — these are conversation shapes, not people, so a coursework-looking chat does not prove anything about who typed it. But it plausibly reflects these tools being pulled toward teaching, explanation, and self-directed study rather than pure workplace output, where Namibia's 35.85% trails the global 43.36%.

The second signature is quieter: Document Processing & Extraction at 5.66% of conversations, 31.0% above the global 4.32%, alongside Office and Administrative Support tasks 31.7% above average at 10.39%. Add the automation lean — 52.91% automation versus 47.09% augmentation, against a global 48.62% — and a coherent picture forms: conversations that ask the model to produce the finished thing. Extract the figures, draft the document, fill the form. Content Creation & Copywriting at 24.21% fits the same pattern. Meanwhile Software Development conversations hold at 10.26%, nearly level with the global 11.51%, while Research & Intelligence dips to 8.42%, 23.0% under the global share. This is one snapshot, so nothing here says where any of it is heading — only what the mix looks like right now.

That mix is useful information if you are building. A strong education-and-content centre of gravity means there is real demand for well-made tools in exactly those spaces, and the gap between using AI and shipping a product built on it is where projects stall.
