In Israel, 16.61% of sampled conversations were matched to software development tasks. In Korea that figure is 10.42%.

## What the data says about Israel

Start with the split that cuts against expectation: in the Israeli sample, 51.94% of conversations lean toward automation — handing the task to the model outright — against 48.06% that lean toward collaborative back-and-forth. Globally the automation share is 48.62%. A reader assuming a tidy rich-country/poor-country ordering might expect heavier hand-holding patterns in some places and lighter ones elsewhere; the Israeli data shows conversations that delegate, decisively, more than the world average. Plausibly this reflects people arriving with a defined job and wanting the output, not a tutorial — though these are conversation shapes, not people, so that reading stays a hedged guess.

The delegation pattern sits on top of an unusually work-heavy mix. 53.32% of sampled conversations were work-related, nearly ten points above the global 43.36%, while coursework-style conversations came in at 9.42% — barely over half the global 16.45%. Education & Learning as a topic runs at 9.29% against 13.23% globally, and conversations matched to "Educational Instruction and Library" tasks at 9.92% against 12.79%. Every educational measure points the same direction: the Israeli sample looks like execution, not study.

Then there is the number that inverts the Korea comparison outright. Software Development makes up 16.61% of Israeli conversations versus Korea's 10.42% — Israel leads by 59.4% in a category many would reflexively assign to Korea. Against the global 11.51%, that is a +44.3% gap, the single most distinctive feature of this country's profile, and conversations matched to "Computer and Mathematical" tasks confirm it at 30.4% versus 23.8% globally. Even the small categories break Israel's way: Hobbies & Lifestyle at 6.83% edges Korea's 6.44% while sitting well under the global 9.49%. Content Creation & Copywriting still tops the list at 18.98%, but below the global 22.72% — the Israeli mix swaps generic content volume for code. Overall intensity lands at an Anthropic Usage Index of 3.05, rank 21 of 121, trailing Korea's 3.78 but with a distinctly engineering-weighted composition.
