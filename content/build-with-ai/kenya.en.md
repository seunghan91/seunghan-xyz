In Kenya, 15.83% of sampled conversations were matched to software development tasks. In Korea that figure is 10.42%.

## What the data says about Kenya

The number that should stop you is this: conversations matched to software development tasks make up 15.83% of Kenya's sample, against 10.42% for Korea — a share 51.9% higher than the country whose usage index is more than ten times larger. Kenya's Anthropic Usage Index sits at 0.34, ranked 100 of 121, while Korea's is 3.78. If you expected a simple ordering where higher adoption means more technical use, the data disagrees. Where usage is lower, it is also more concentrated: fewer casual conversations, more ones that look like building things.

That concentration shows up everywhere in the Kenyan numbers. 51.15% of conversations are work-related, well above the global 43.36%. Conversations matched to education and learning tasks run at 15.93%, versus 11.21% in Korea and 13.23% globally. Content creation and copywriting leads the topic list at 29.57%, against a global 22.72%. Taken together, the plausible reading is that people opening these tools in Kenya arrive with a task already in hand — something to write, something to learn, something to ship — rather than to browse what the model can do. That is a guess from the shape of the data, not a claim about who those users are.

Two gaps are just as telling. Research and intelligence tasks account for only 7.29% of conversations, a third below the global 10.94%, and the automation-leaning share is 54.61% against 45.39% augmentation — more delegation, less back-and-forth than the global average of 48.62% automation. One way to read that pair: less open-ended exploration, more direct requests to produce an output. Combined with the coursework-shaped share of 18.8% (global 16.45%), the pattern is of conversations oriented toward finishing concrete work.

None of this says anything about employment or who is on the other side of the keyboard — these are conversations matched to job tasks, not people's jobs. But as a snapshot of how the tools get used, Kenya's profile is unusually practical.
