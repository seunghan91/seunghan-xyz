In Qatar, 9.18% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about Qatar

Fifty point eight five percent. That is the share of sampled conversations from Qatar that are about work — against a global average of 43.36%. If you assumed usage patterns would simply track the Anthropic Usage Index, where Qatar sits at 1.66 (41st of 121 countries) while Korea posts 3.78, this number cuts the other way. Fewer conversations per capita than Korea, yes — but a substantially larger fraction of them aimed at getting work done. Whatever drives the volume, the intent skews practical.

The topic mix fills in the picture. Content creation and copywriting leads at 24.25% of conversations, research and intelligence takes 11.2%, and conversations matched to software development tasks come in at 10.15% — a shade under the global 11.51%. The coursework share, 11.65%, runs well below the global 16.45%. And the single most distinctive tilt in the whole dataset is office and administrative support: 9.26% of conversations matched those job tasks, 17.4% above the global 7.89%. A cautious reading — and it is only a reading — is that a lot of drafting, coordinating, and document-heavy work in Doha already flows through these tools, treated as instruments rather than curiosities.

Two comparisons with Korea deserve stating plainly, because Qatar is ahead in both. Hobbies and lifestyle accounts for 9.18% of conversations versus Korea's 6.44% — 42.5% higher. Education and learning sits at 12.72% against Korea's 11.21%. Meanwhile the automation-versus-augmentation split, 48.08% to 51.92%, lands almost exactly on the global 48.62% — no unusual appetite for handing tasks over wholesale, just a balanced mix of delegation and collaboration. Put together, this is not a usage profile organised around study or experimentation. It is organised around output, with genuine room left over for personal interests.

That profile maps directly onto the problems I like working on. When conversations are weighted toward shipping rather than exploring, the sticking points tend to be concrete: getting an app through App Store or Play Store review without a rejection spiral, making AI agents and MCP tooling behave outside a demo, backend and auth done properly the first time, mobile development across iOS and Android, and the reliability engineering that keeps something alive once people depend on it. The Seoul–Doha time difference is workable, the help is free and informal, and the data suggests you are already mid-build — so bring the one thing that is stuck.
