In Mauritius, 16.48% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Mauritius

A 47% gap is the place to start. Conversations from Mauritius matched to education and learning tasks make up 16.48% of the sample; in Korea the same category sits at 11.21%. Anyone assuming a neat ordering where the higher-income, higher-usage country leads on every dimension would have that assumption broken right here. Korea's overall usage index is 3.78 against Mauritius's 1.74 (38th of 121 countries), so the island generates a thinner stream of conversations per person — but a thicker share of that stream is spent learning something. Against the global average of 13.23%, the Mauritian share runs 24.6% higher, the single largest positive deviation in the country's profile. Coursework-flavoured conversations overall come in at 17.84%, also above the global 16.45%.

That leaning shows up in how the tools get used, not just what they get asked about. Work conversations account for 49.36% of the sample versus 43.36% globally, and within them, conversations matched to office and administrative support tasks reach 10.34% against a global 7.89% — a 31.1% edge. Sales-related tasks run the other way, 7.47% versus 9.14%. For an island economy built heavily on finance and services, conducted across English, French, and Creole, a plausible reading is that these tools are being pointed at documents, correspondence, and the administrative layer of business — though that is an inference from the shape of the data, not a fact about who is typing. The automation-versus-augmentation split tilts slightly toward handing tasks over completely: 50.62% to 49.38%, where globally it is 48.62%.

Content creation and copywriting tops the topic list at 23.62%, close to the global 22.72%. The more interesting entry is software development: 12.21% of conversations, ahead of the global 11.51% and ahead of Korea's 10.42% by 17.2%. Mauritius leads Korea on three of the measured topics, and two of those leads are substantial. Meanwhile hobbies and lifestyle sits at 7.07%, a quarter below the global 9.49% — the largest negative deviation in the profile. The composite picture is usage weighted toward output: writing, building, studying, with relatively little left over for play.
