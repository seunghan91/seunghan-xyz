In Latvia, 12.5% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about Latvia

Latvia's most telling number is the one that has nothing to do with work. Conversations matched to Hobbies & Lifestyle tasks make up 12.5% of the country's sampled usage — 31.7% above the global share of 9.49%, and 94.1% higher than Korea's 6.44%. Someone assuming usage patterns simply track national wealth would not predict that a small Baltic state out-hobbies one of the most wired countries in Asia by nearly two to one. What it plausibly reflects is a population treating these tools as a general-purpose instrument rather than office equipment: the same conversations that handle work also handle personal projects, experiments, and curiosity. That is an inference from the mix, not a fact about individuals — but the mix is what it is.

The supporting numbers lean the same way. Work conversations account for 38.17% of Latvia's total, well under the global 43.36%, while coursework-shaped conversations run at 18.58% against a global 16.45% — and that says the dialogues looked like learning, not that the people behind them were anyone in particular. Research & Intelligence is the second-most distinctive category at 12.62% versus a global 10.94%. Add those together and the profile is of conversations weighted toward finding things out and making things for their own sake, with the purely occupational slice unusually thin.

Against Korea specifically, Latvia leads in all three categories where the two are directly compared: Hobbies & Lifestyle by that wide margin, Education & Learning 11.72% to 11.21%, and Software Development 10.61% to 10.42% — conversations matched to software development tasks are, proportionally, slightly more common in Latvia than in the country Seunghan works from. Its overall usage index of 2.37 (27th of 121 countries) sits below Korea's 3.78, and its automation-to-augmentation split of 47.7% to 52.3% tracks the global balance almost exactly, so the signature here is not intensity or tooling style but subject matter.

That profile — builders whose conversations already span research, learning, and software tasks, much of it self-directed — maps closely onto what Seunghan can actually help with. If a hobby project in Latvia is reaching the point where it should become a shipped app, he can help get it into the stores. If the work involves AI agents or MCP tooling, backend and auth decisions, mobile development, or making a system reliable enough to trust, those are the areas he works in daily. The help is free and informal, Seoul and Latvia are only a few hours apart, and the conversation can start wherever a project currently stands.
