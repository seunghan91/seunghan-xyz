In Finland, 14.23% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about Finland

Finland's most distinctive number is not about work at all: 14.23% of sampled conversations matched Hobbies & Lifestyle tasks — about half again the global average of 9.49%, and more than double Korea's 6.44%. A reader assuming that heavier AI use means heavier office use would not predict this. One plausible reading, hedged as a guess: in a country with long winters and a strong culture of personal projects, people are bringing these tools into garages, kitchens, and cabins as much as into offices. The second-strongest signal points the same direction — Research & Intelligence tasks take 14.22% of conversations, 30% above the global 10.94%, and ahead of Korea's 13.46%.

The work numbers fill in the picture. Only 38.49% of Finland's conversations matched work tasks, against a global 43.36%, and coursework-like conversations came in at 12.85% versus 16.45% globally. Within work-matched conversations, the tilt is away from routine: Office and Administrative Support tasks sit at 6.39% against a global 7.89%, while Sales and Related tasks run at 10.84% against 9.14%. And on the automation–augmentation axis, Finland leans toward keeping a person in the loop — 54.09% of conversations looked like augmentation versus 45.91% automation, where globally automation takes 48.62%.

Software development conversations hold 10.6% of the sample, essentially at the global 11.51% and a notch above Korea's 10.42%. That last comparison deserves plain statement: by this measure, conversations matched to software development tasks are slightly more prominent in Finland than in one of the most wired countries on earth. Content Creation & Copywriting tops the topic list at 19.6%, though under the global 22.72%, while Education & Learning at 9.15% runs about 31% below the global share.

One caveat holds for all of this: it is a single snapshot of conversations, not of people or jobs, and it says nothing about direction over time. What it does say is that Finland's usage is already broad, curiosity-driven, and hands-on.
