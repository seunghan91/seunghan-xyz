## What the data says about Bosnia and Herzegovina

Bosnia and Herzegovina ranks 77th of 121 countries on the Anthropic Usage Index, with a score of 0.61 against Korea's 3.78. By volume alone, that looks like a modest footprint. But look at what fills the conversations rather than how many there are, and the ordering flips: 15.4% of sampled conversations matched to software development tasks, compared with 11.51% globally and 10.42% in Korea. A country using these tools at a fraction of Korea's intensity is devoting a much larger slice of that use to building software — nearly half again as much, proportionally.

The lead over Korea does not stop there. Conversations matched to hobbies and lifestyle topics make up 10.96% in Bosnia and Herzegovina versus 6.44% in Korea, a 70% edge, and well above the global 9.49%. One plausible reading, hedged as any reading of a single snapshot must be: usage here skews toward personal projects and hands-on making rather than institutional or administrative work. That fits the other distinctive numbers. Office and administrative support tasks account for only 6.51% of conversations, against a global 7.89%, and education and learning conversations sit at 11.2% versus 13.23% globally. Coursework-style conversations overall are 14.91%, a touch under the worldwide 16.45%.

Elsewhere, the country is almost uncannily average. Work-related conversations are 43.38%, the global figure is 43.36%. Content creation and copywriting leads the topic list at 22.98%, matching the global 22.72% to within a rounding error. The automation-versus-augmentation split runs 50.91% to 49.09%, slightly more automation-leaning than the global 48.62% but the same basic shape. Nothing here suggests an outlier in how the tools are being approached — only in how often the conversations turn toward code and personal builds.

Two cautions belong with all of this. These figures describe conversations matched to job tasks, not the jobs people hold, and the data says nothing about who the users are. And this is one snapshot: it cannot show whether anything is rising or falling, only where things stand.
