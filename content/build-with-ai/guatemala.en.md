## What the data says about Guatemala

Guatemala's conversations with Claude look more like studying than working. The coursework and learning share of sampled conversations is 32.03% — double the global 16.45% — while the work share is 39.3%, below the global 43.36%. For a country whose Anthropic Usage Index is 0.37 (rank 97 of 121, against Korea's 3.78), anyone expecting a simple rich-country/poor-country gradient in how seriously the tool gets used would be surprised by that. The conversations are not lighter or more casual than the global norm. They are more academic in shape, by the widest margin in the country's profile.

Two cautions belong with that number. First, it describes conversations, not the people having them: a conversation that looks like coursework means the exchange resembled coursework, full stop. Second, this is a single snapshot, so nothing here says anything about direction or speed. What the figure plausibly reflects, as a guess grounded in the rest of the data, is a usage pattern where the tool is treated as something to learn with — an instrument for building understanding — alongside a substantial block of practical work, not as a shortcut around either.

The Korea comparisons make this concrete. Education & Learning as a topic takes 18.88% of Guatemala's sampled conversations versus 11.21% in Korea — 68.4% higher in the country with a tenth of Korea's overall usage. Conversations matched to software development tasks run 12.34% in Guatemala against Korea's 10.42%, and hobbies and lifestyle conversations 7.57% against 6.44%. Guatemala leads Korea in all three, plainly. Against the global average, the education premium is +42.7%, the largest positive deviation in the profile, while research and intelligence runs 33.9% under the global share (7.23% versus 10.94%) and hobbies and lifestyle 20.2% under.

The work that does show up leans toward getting finished output. Automation-leaning conversations edge augmentation 51.75% to 48.25% (global automation: 48.62%), conversations matched to office and administrative support tasks run 9.69% against a global 7.89%, and content creation and copywriting tops the topic list at 23.75%, almost exactly the global 22.72%. Sales-related tasks come in at 7.33% versus a global 9.14%. Put together: produce the thing, learn the thing, skip the open-ended research.
