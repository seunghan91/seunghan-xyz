## What the data says about Australia

Australia sits at the very top of the Anthropic Usage Index: 6.4, ranked 1 of 121 countries measured. Korea, for comparison, records 3.78 — a strong figure, and still well short of Australia's. On a per-capita basis, nobody in the dataset talks to Claude more than Australians do. If you assumed AI usage would follow some tidy ordering of expected tech leaders, this is the number that breaks the assumption, and it breaks it in Australia's favour.

The shape of that usage is worth reading closely. Work conversations account for 42.67%, almost exactly the global 43.36%, so this is not casual experimentation. The skew shows up in learning: 20.69% of conversations are matched to coursework or learning tasks against a global 16.45%, and the Education & Learning topic appears in 16.08% of sampled conversations versus 13.23% globally — a 21.5% overrepresentation, the country's largest positive deviation. Australia also leads Korea here concretely: 16.08% to Korea's 11.21% on Education & Learning, and 9.14% to 6.44% on Hobbies & Lifestyle. A hedged reading: these tools are being used heavily as instruments for learning and for life outside work, not only as workplace machinery.

The counterweight is software development. Conversations matched to software development tasks make up 8.32% of the Australian sample against 11.51% globally — a 27.7% shortfall, the biggest gap in either direction in the country's profile. Content creation leads the topic list at 23.53%, research follows at 11.32%, and the automation-versus-augmentation split tilts collaborative: 54.5% augmentation versus 45.5% automation, slightly more so than the global 48.62% automation share. Taken together, the snapshot shows very heavy overall usage weighted toward writing, research, and study, with engineering conversations comparatively thin next to everything else.
