In Puerto Rico, 11.67% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about Puerto Rico

The number that breaks the expected ordering is the split between automation and augmentation. Conversations from Puerto Rico lean 49.92% toward automation and 50.08% toward augmentation — essentially a coin flip, and a touch more automation-leaning than the global split of 48.62%. Someone assuming that lighter AI use means more tentative, back-and-forth usage would have it backwards here: with an Anthropic Usage Index of 1.68 (rank 40 of 121), well below Korea's 3.78, Puerto Rico's conversations are already slightly more directive and hands-off than the global average. What that plausibly reflects is that when people there sit down with these tools, they tend to arrive with a concrete job to finish and let the model run with it, rather than treating it as a collaborator to be negotiated with.

The clearest place Puerto Rico leads Korea is Hobbies & Lifestyle: 11.67% of sampled conversations, against Korea's 6.44% — 81.2% higher, and 23.0% above the global average of 9.49%. Education & Learning follows at 13.63%, versus Korea's 11.21% and a global 13.23%. Taken together with Content Creation & Copywriting at 21.5%, the picture is of usage weighted toward creative and personal projects — writing, learning, and making things outside a job description. Conversations matched to coursework or learning tasks account for 16.1%, right at the global 16.45%, so the education tilt shows up in topic share rather than in any unusual coursework-like pattern.

Just as telling is what is low. Conversations matched to software development tasks make up 7.54% of the sample, against a global 11.51% — a 34.5% shortfall, the territory's single most distinctive gap. Work conversations overall sit at 40.99% versus 43.36% globally, and conversations matched to Arts, Design, Entertainment, Sports, and Media job tasks run 11.37% versus 13.55%. The pattern is consistent: less code, less formal work, more personal creation. Whether that reflects a tool culture built around side projects, small ventures, or creative pursuits is a guess the data cannot settle — but the gap is large enough to be real.
