In Estonia, 13.03% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about Estonia

Here is a number that cuts against the obvious ordering: conversations matched to software development tasks make up 11.39% of Estonia's sample, ahead of Korea's 10.42% — even though Korea's overall usage index sits higher at 3.78 against Estonia's 3.21, which already ranks Estonia 19th of 121 countries. Lower overall intensity, more engineering in the mix. For anyone who assumed the country with the bigger index would lead on every technical dimension, Estonia's profile says otherwise.

What fills the rest of the sample is arguably more telling. Conversations matched to Hobbies & Lifestyle tasks reach 13.03%, versus a global 9.49% and Korea's 6.44% — a 102% gap over Korea, the largest lead in the data. Only 40.38% of Estonian conversations are work-related, below the global 43.36%, and coursework-looking conversations are 12.67%, under the global 16.45%. Read together — and this is inference, not fact — the balance tilts toward personal projects and self-directed building rather than assignments or office tasks. The automation split leans the same way: 49.22% of conversations ask the tool to do the thing, slightly above the global 48.62%, against 50.78% for back-and-forth augmentation. Delegation over dialogue, if only by a shade.

There is also a commercial undercurrent. Conversations matched to sales-related tasks account for 11.43%, about 25% above the global 9.14%, while Research & Intelligence runs at 12.81% versus 10.94% globally. Content creation tops the table at 19.73%, though below the global 22.72%. The outline that emerges — hedged, since one snapshot cannot show direction — is of conversations that build something, research it, and then try to put it in front of people. Worth repeating: these figures describe conversations matched to task categories, never the jobs or identities of the people having them.
