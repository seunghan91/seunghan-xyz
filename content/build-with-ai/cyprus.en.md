## What the data says about Cyprus

A usage index of 3.08, ranking 20th out of 121 countries measured, is the number that should rearrange assumptions. Cyprus is a small island in the eastern Mediterranean, and yet by this measure it sits in the top sixth of the world for intensity of AI use — not far behind Korea's 3.78, a country that treats software as national infrastructure. Anyone expecting a tidy ordering where usage maps neatly onto country size or income will not find it here.

What those conversations are about matters more than the volume. In the Cypriot sample, 52.16% of conversations matched work tasks, against a global share of 43.36%, while coursework-like conversations ran at just 9.11% — barely over half the global 16.45%. Education & Learning topics show the sharpest negative deviation in the whole profile: 9.38% versus a global 13.23%. One careful way to read this is that the tools show up in Cyprus as instruments for doing rather than studying — conversations that look like deliverables, not assignments. That is an inference from task patterns, not a claim about who is typing; the data describes conversations, never the people behind them.

Cyprus also runs ahead of Korea in two specific categories, and that deserves plain statement. Conversations matched to software development tasks account for 12.13% of the Cypriot sample versus 10.42% of the Korean one. Conversations matched to hobbies and lifestyle tasks are 9.63% in Cyprus against Korea's 6.44% — nearly half again as large a share. The strongest positive deviation from the global average is Research & Intelligence at 13.34%, about 22% above the worldwide figure. Meanwhile the automation-versus-augmentation split — 47.74% to 52.26% — sits almost exactly on the global balance, so nothing here suggests delegation run wild; the mix leans, if anything, toward keeping a person in the loop.

That combination — research-heavy, work-weighted, with a real software development contingent — is precisely the territory I work in from Seoul. The time difference means a question sent in the Cypriot evening lands in my morning, which suits asynchronous collaboration well. If you are shipping an app to the stores, building AI agents or wiring up MCP tooling, sorting out a backend and its auth, deep in mobile development, or trying to make a service stay reliably up, I am happy to dig in with you — free, informal, and peer to peer.
