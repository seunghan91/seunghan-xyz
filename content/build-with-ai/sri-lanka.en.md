## What the data says about Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka's Anthropic Usage Index sits at 0.56, ranking 81 of 121 countries, while Korea's is 3.78. Read only those two numbers and you would expect Korea to dominate every category of use. The data says otherwise. Conversations from Sri Lanka devoted to Education & Learning make up 22.9% of the sample, against Korea's 11.21% — 104.3% higher. Conversations matched to software development tasks are 18.42% in Sri Lanka versus 10.42% in Korea — 76.8% higher. The single most interesting fact in this country's data is that the lower-volume country leads the higher-volume one in the two categories most associated with building things.

The texture of that lead is worth examining. More than a third of sampled conversations — 34.98% — look like coursework or learning, over twice the global share of 16.45%. Work conversations, by contrast, are 38.98%, below the global 43.36%. I want to be precise about what this means: the figures describe what conversations resembled, not who was having them. A conversation that looks like coursework tells us the text looked like coursework, nothing more. With that caution, a plausible reading is that people in Sri Lanka reach for these tools with an unusual concentration of intent — less as a general-purpose office assistant, more as a working environment for technical material.

The occupational-task matching supports that reading. Computer and Mathematical tasks appear in 29.65% of conversations versus a global 23.8%, while Sales and Related tasks run at 6.09% against a global 9.14%. Research & Intelligence is also lower than average — 6.76% versus 10.94% globally. Content Creation & Copywriting leads the topic list at 24.48%, close to the global 22.72%, but Education & Learning at 22.9% (global 13.23%) and Software Development at 18.42% (global 11.51%) are where Sri Lanka most clearly departs from the average profile. The automation-versus-augmentation split tilts slightly toward automation — 52.19% to 47.81%, against a global 48.62% automation — consistent with conversations aimed at getting concrete technical output rather than refining drafts.

This is one snapshot, with no trend line behind it, so I will not claim any of this is rising or falling. What it shows plainly is a conversation mix that a simple rich-country/poor-country ordering would never have predicted.
