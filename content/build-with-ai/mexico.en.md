In Mexico, 9.36% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about Mexico

The number that should stop you is this: Mexico's Anthropic Usage Index sits at 0.63, putting it 76th of 121 countries, while Korea — where I live — clocks 3.78. By that measure, roughly six times less AI conversation volume per person. And yet Mexican conversations matched to software development tasks make up 12.47% of the sample, against Korea's 10.42%. Education and learning tasks: 14.42% versus Korea's 11.21%. Hobbies and lifestyle: 9.36% versus 6.44%, a full 45% higher. Lower volume, but the volume that exists is pointed squarely at building and learning. That reads to me like a user base that opens these tools with intent rather than out of ambient novelty — though that is my interpretation of the shape, not something the numbers prove.

The coursework angle sharpens the picture. Conversations that look like coursework or learning make up 21.08% of Mexican conversations, well above the global 16.45% and the second-highest share in the sample. Important caveat: that describes what the conversations looked like, not who was having them. A conversation that resembles coursework can come from anyone teaching themselves anything. What it does suggest is that a large slice of usage is structured learning — the kind of dialogue where someone is working through material, not just extracting an answer.

Work-matched conversations account for 44.97%, slightly above the global 43.36%, and the automation-versus-augmentation split sits at 50.36% to 49.64% — close to the global balance, with a slight lean toward handing tasks over rather than collaborating on them. Content creation and copywriting tops the topic list at 24.01%, followed by education and software development.

The strangest figure is research and intelligence: 8.39% against a global 10.94%, putting Mexico 23% below average on the one category where most countries cluster upward. I won't pretend to know why. But combined with everything above, the pattern is consistent — Mexican conversations skew toward making things and learning things, away from analysis and information gathering.
