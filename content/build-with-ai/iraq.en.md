In Iraq, 26.49% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Iraq

Iraq sits at rank 106 of 121 on the Anthropic Usage Index, with a score of 0.24 against Korea's 3.78 — and yet its conversations are more heavily weighted toward software development than Korea's. Conversations matched to software development tasks account for 12.52% of Iraq's sample, ahead of both the global average of 11.51% and Korea's 10.42%. That 20.2% edge over Korea is the detail that breaks any simple assumption about what usage looks like in a country this far down the overall index.

The dominant signal in the data, though, is learning. Just over a third of Iraq's conversations — 34.54% — look like coursework, more than twice the global share of 16.45%. Education & Learning is the single biggest request topic at 26.49%, exactly double the global 13.23%, and conversations matched to "Educational Instruction and Library" job tasks run at 18.23% against a global 12.79%. Two different cuts of the data point the same direction, so this is probably not noise. A plausible reading is that people in Iraq are using these tools as study partners and tutors — asking for explanations, walking through problems, building skills — though the data alone cannot say who is doing it or why.

The flip side of that pattern is a work share of 23.54%, barely half the global 43.36%. Whatever else these conversations are, most of them are not delegated office tasks. Content Creation & Copywriting, at 16.38%, sits 27.9% below the global 22.72%, and Research & Intelligence, at 7.73%, sits 29.3% below the global 10.94%. In style of use, meanwhile, Iraq is almost perfectly average: 49.44% of conversations lean toward automation and 50.56% toward augmentation, against a global automation figure of 48.62%. Nothing unusual there — the distinctiveness is all in subject matter.

Iraq also leads Korea in two other categories worth naming plainly. Education & Learning runs 136.3% higher than Korea's 11.21% share, and Hobbies & Lifestyle at 9.83% is 52.6% above Korea's 6.44%. Lower overall volume, in Iraq's case, does not mean shallower engagement.
