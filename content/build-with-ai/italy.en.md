In Italy, 10.14% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about Italy

Italy's headline number is modest: an Anthropic Usage Index of 1.7, ranked 39 of 121, against Korea's 3.78. The interesting number hides one level down. Conversations matched to software development tasks make up 11.46% of Italy's sample — ahead of Korea's 10.42% and effectively tied with the global 11.51%. Conversations that look like education and learning come to 12.48%, again ahead of Korea's 11.21%. And hobbies and lifestyle conversations reach 10.14%, a full 57.5% above Korea's 6.44%. Anyone assuming the country with the bigger index must also have the more technical conversation mix would be wrong on all three counts.

What that plausibly reflects is concentration of intent. With lighter overall usage but a mix tilted toward code, coursework-shaped problem solving, and personal projects, the conversations happening in Italy look less like casual experimentation and more like people arriving with something specific to make or figure out. That is a reading of the shape, not a fact the data states outright. The rest of the topic table fits it: content creation and copywriting leads at 22.97%, almost exactly the global 22.72%, with research and intelligence at 10.51% — a broad, even spread rather than one dominant use.

On the measures where Italy does not stand out, it hugs the global line closely. Work-matched conversations are 42.89% against a global 43.36%, and coursework-looking conversations are 16.19% against 16.45%. The one genuine lean is in style of use: 46.36% of Italian conversations lean toward automation while 53.64% lean toward augmentation, a slightly stronger preference for working alongside the model than the global automation share of 48.62% would predict. More dialogue, less delegation.

These are conversations matched to task types — they say nothing about who is having them or what anyone does for a living, and one snapshot cannot speak to direction. What they do show is that Italy's conversation mix already centers on the work I do every day in Seoul and enjoy helping with informally, for free, wherever the builder happens to be: shipping apps to the App Store or Google Play, building AI agent and MCP tooling, getting backend and auth right, mobile development, and the reliability engineering that keeps a project alive after launch. If you are in Italy and mid-build on any of that, the conversation mix says you are in good company — and my inbox is open.
