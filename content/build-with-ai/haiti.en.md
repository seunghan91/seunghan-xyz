In Haiti, 30.38% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Haiti

The number that should reorder your assumptions: conversations from Haiti matched to software development tasks make up 11.96% of sampled usage — slightly ahead of Korea's 10.42%. Korea's overall Anthropic Usage Index is 3.78, one of the highest measured, while Haiti's is 0.14, near the bottom of the 121 countries ranked. Volume and composition are different things. Whatever is happening in Haiti's smaller pool of conversations, building software is at least as prominent a use of these tools there as it is in one of the most saturated AI markets in the world.

The composition elsewhere is striking in its own way. Education and learning topics account for 30.38% of sampled conversations, more than double the global share of 13.23% and 171% above Korea's 11.21%. Coursework-shaped conversations are 29.3% of the total against a global 16.45%, and conversations matched to educational instruction and library job tasks run 52% above the global average. The data doesn't say who is on the other end of these sessions — a high coursework share means the conversations looked like coursework, nothing more. What it does suggest is that the tools are being leaned on heavily as a teaching and explanation layer, alongside content creation (27%, modestly above the global 22.72%) and a creative-arts task share 39.5% above the global norm.

Work-oriented conversations are 29.88% of the total, well under the global 43.36%, and research-and-intelligence topics sit at 5.21%, roughly half the global 10.94%. Automation-leaning use edges out augmentation 52.26% to 47.74%, slightly more delegation-heavy than the global split — handing whole tasks over rather than collaborating line by line. Read together, the plausible picture is of usage concentrated on learning something, making something, and shipping something, with relatively little of the corporate analysis workload that dominates higher-index countries. That reading is an inference from category shares, not a fact about any individual user.
