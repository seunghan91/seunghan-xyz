In Panama, 16.6% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Panama

Panama's conversations with AI look more like coursework than Korea's do. That is the number worth leading with, because it cuts against any lazy assumption about which countries use these tools "seriously": 22.08% of sampled conversations in Panama matched coursework or learning tasks, against a global average of 16.45%, and among the top request topics Education & Learning takes 16.6% — fully 48.1% higher than Korea's 11.21%, in a country whose overall usage index (3.78) is nearly triple Panama's 1.28. On this dimension, Panama does not trail the comparison country. It is well out in front.

That does not tell us who is having these conversations, and the data should not be stretched that far. What it does say is that when people in Panama open the tool, a striking share of what they bring looks like studying, practicing, and working through material. The learning skew shows up twice — once in the coursework share, once in the topic mix (16.6% versus a global 13.23%) — so it is unlikely to be noise. A plausible reading is that the tool is being used heavily as an explainer and tutor, alongside its more transactional uses.

The transactional uses are there too, and they have their own Panamanian signature. Work conversations are 44.87% of the total, a touch above the global 43.36%, and within the job-task matching, office and administrative support conversations run at 9.39% versus a global 7.89% — the clearest workplace over-index in the data. Content creation and copywriting leads all topics at 25.23%, also above the global 22.72%. For a trade-and-services economy sitting between two oceans and two languages, a tilt toward writing, correspondence, and administrative work is easy to believe, though the data itself only shows the conversations, not the industries behind them.

Just as telling is what Panama is not over-indexed on. Software development conversations sit at 10.1%, under the global 11.51%, and research and intelligence at 8.82%, under the global 10.94% — the two biggest negative deviations. Hobbies and lifestyle is also below the global share (7.67% versus 9.49%), yet still 19.1% above Korea's 6.44%. The automation-augmentation split, 50.85% to 49.15%, is slightly more delegation-leaning than the global 48.62%.
