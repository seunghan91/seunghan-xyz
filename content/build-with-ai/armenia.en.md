## What the data says about Armenia

A reader scanning the headline figures might expect a familiar story: Armenia's Anthropic Usage Index sits at 0.95, ranked 63rd of 121 countries, while Korea's stands at 3.78 — roughly four times the intensity. The surprise arrives one level down. Of the conversations sampled from Armenia, 16.25% were matched to software development tasks. In Korea, that figure is 10.42%. On the most technical category in the dataset, Armenia's share runs 56% higher than the country using these tools four times as much. Against the global average of 11.51%, Armenia's share is 41.2% above baseline — the single most distinctive feature of its profile.

The lead over Korea is not confined to code. Conversations matched to hobbies and lifestyle tasks account for 9.44% of Armenia's sample versus 6.44% of Korea's, a 46.6% higher share, and education and learning conversations edge ahead too, 12.87% to 11.21%. Whatever the gap in raw volume, the composition of Armenia's usage tilts toward making things — software, side interests, study — rather than away from it.

The work-versus-learning split adds texture. 46.18% of conversations matched work tasks, above the global 43.36%, while coursework-style conversations came in at 14.08%, below the global 16.45%. Note what these figures actually describe: the shape of conversations, not the identities of the people having them. A work-heavy, coursework-light mix says the typical session from Armenia looked like a task someone needed done, and that is all it says — but paired with the software development share, a plausible reading is that usage in Armenia concentrates around concrete building work, where an assistant earns its keep in output rather than exploration. That is inference from the data, and it is hedged accordingly.

On the automation question Armenia looks almost exactly like the world: 48% of conversations leaned toward delegating tasks outright, 52% toward collaborative use, against a global automation figure of 48.62%. Meanwhile content creation and copywriting tops the topic list at 22.84%, statistically indistinguishable from the global 22.72%. The ordinary uses are all present in ordinary proportion; it is the outsized technical core that sets the country's data apart.
