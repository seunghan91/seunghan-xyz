## What the data says about Germany

Germany's usage index is 2.4, placing it 26th of 121 countries — well below Korea's 3.78. If you assumed that ranking would map onto intensity of engagement, the topic mix argues otherwise. The single most striking figure is Hobbies & Lifestyle: 12.07% of sampled German conversations, 27.2% above the global average of 9.49% and a full 87.4% higher than Korea's 6.44%. A country that looks middling on the headline index is, on this dimension, ahead of one of the heaviest AI-using countries in the dataset. Plausibly — and this is inference, not measurement — that reflects a user base that brings self-directed, personal projects to these tools rather than only delegated work.

The work numbers support that reading. Just 37.62% of German conversations are matched to work tasks, against a global 43.36%, and conversations matched to coursework or learning sit at 13.65%, below the global 16.45%. Meanwhile Research & Intelligence runs at 12.66% versus 10.94% globally — a 15.7% premium — and conversations matched to sales-related tasks come in at 10.77% versus 9.14%. Content Creation & Copywriting, the top category nearly everywhere, is actually underrepresented in Germany at 20.75% against 22.72% worldwide. Whatever is happening, it tilts away from pure output production and toward investigation and personal making.

The collaboration style adds a consistent texture. German conversations lean less toward automation than the global pattern — 45.22% automation versus a global 48.62%, with augmentation at 54.78%. And on Education & Learning topics, Germany edges Korea 11.33% to 11.21% — a narrow lead, but a lead. These figures describe conversations matched to task categories, not the people having them, and a single snapshot says nothing about direction over time. Software Development, at 10.19% against a global 11.51%, simply means software-shaped problems arrive at the tool slightly less often than average — not that anyone is or isn't a developer.
