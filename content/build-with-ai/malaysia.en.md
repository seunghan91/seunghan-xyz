## What the data says about Malaysia

The number worth starting with is 12.99%. That is the share of Malaysian sampled conversations matched to software development tasks — above the global 11.51%, and ahead of Korea's 10.42% by nearly a quarter. Set that against the usage index, where Malaysia sits at 0.87, ranked 67 of 121, next to Korea's 3.78. Anyone expecting the higher-index country to dominate every column would be wrong on this one: a smaller overall volume of conversations is paired with a mix that leans harder into actually building software. A plausible reading is that Malaysian conversations concentrate on applied work rather than spreading thinly across casual use — though the data shows the pattern, not the cause.

The lean is consistent elsewhere. Conversations matched to work account for 46.28% of the sample versus 43.36% globally. Conversations that look like coursework or learning run at 19.82% against 16.45% globally, and the Education & Learning topic category reaches 13.89% — ahead of the global 13.23% and 23.9% above Korea's 11.21%. The automation-augmentation balance, 47.2% to 52.8%, tilts slightly further toward augmentation than the global 48.62% automation figure. Read together, the profile is one of conversations that extend and sharpen work already underway: drafting, explaining, debugging, iterating.

The most distinctive figure is a gap rather than a surplus. Hobbies & Lifestyle takes 7.11% of Malaysian conversations, roughly 25% below the global 9.49% — the largest divergence from the global average in the whole profile. Content Creation & Copywriting leads the topic table at 24.3%, close to the global 22.72%, while Research & Intelligence runs slightly under at 9.94% versus 10.94%. What fills the space left by the lifestyle shortfall is production: copy, coursework-shaped questions, and code.
