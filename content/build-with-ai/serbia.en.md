## What the data says about Serbia

The surprise in Serbia's data is where the conversations point. Conversations matched to software development tasks make up 15.99% of the sampled total — 38.9% above the global average of 11.51% — even though Serbia's overall Anthropic Usage Index is 1.31, ranked 53 of 121, against Korea's 3.78. A naive reading would expect lower overall usage to mean a thinner, more casual mix of requests. The opposite shows up here: the conversation mix is more technical than in countries with far heavier aggregate usage, Korea included, where software development tasks account for 10.42% of conversations. Serbia's share is 53.5% higher. What that plausibly reflects is a selection effect in how the tool gets used — conversations that skew toward concrete, build-oriented work rather than spreading evenly across every possible topic. That reading is a guess from the shape of the numbers, not a claim about anyone, but the skew itself is plainly in the data.

The texture of the conversations adds to it. Work-related conversations come in at 46.02%, above the global 43.36%, while coursework- and learning-flavoured conversations sit at 11.66%, well under the global 16.45%. The Education & Learning topic mirrors that: 10.71% against a global 13.23%, a 19% discount. And the split between handing work off versus working alongside the tool tilts toward collaboration — augmentation at 54.41% against automation at 45.59%, where globally automation runs higher at 48.62%. Conversations matched to office and administrative support job tasks, at 6.12% versus 7.89% globally, are the clearest below-average signal in the set. Taken together, the numbers sketch requests that look like making things, with the tool as a working partner, rather than delegating routine desk work or studying for an exam.

Serbia also leads Korea somewhere less expected: Hobbies & Lifestyle accounts for 11.13% of conversations, 72.8% higher than Korea's 6.44% and 17.3% above the global 9.49%. Alongside the software development lead, that pairing — code and personal projects — gives Serbia's profile a distinct texture that neither a pure work tool nor a pure study aid would produce. Content Creation & Copywriting, at 21.09%, and Research & Intelligence, at 11.38%, both sit near their global averages, so the distinctiveness is concentrated exactly in those two categories.
