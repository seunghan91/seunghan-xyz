## What the data says about Mozambique

Forty-three percent. That is the share of sampled conversations from Mozambique that look like coursework or learning — 43.32%, against a global average of 16.45%. If you came in assuming the data would line up neatly with national income, it does not. Mozambique's overall Anthropic Usage Index sits at 0.08, rank 120 of 121, so the volume of conversations is small — but their shape is unlike anywhere else. Work conversations are 31.88% here versus 43.36% globally, and the learning share is nearly triple the world figure. These numbers describe conversations matched to task types, not the people having them, so I will not speculate about who is behind the keyboard. What the pattern plausibly reflects — and this is a reasoned guess — is a usage profile weighted toward skill-building and output production rather than casual delegation.

The automation split reinforces that reading. Mozambique-registered conversations lean toward automation 52.13% of the time against 47.87% augmentation, while the global split tilts the other way at 48.62% automation. When conversations happen here, they tend to hand the model a complete task more often than the world average does.

Now the comparisons that matter to me, sitting in Seoul. On Education & Learning, Mozambique conversations run at 26.48% while Korea's sit at 11.21% — Mozambique is 136.2% higher, full stop. On Content Creation & Copywriting, the single largest topic here at 31.89% of conversations, Mozambique also edges Korea's 27.3%. Document Processing & Extraction is another standout: 8.04% of conversations versus a global 4.32%, an 86.1% premium over the world average. And conversations matched to arts, design, entertainment, sports, and media tasks make up 19.86% here against 13.55% globally — a creative signature that pairs naturally with that copywriting lead. Research & Intelligence, at 4.91% versus 10.94% globally, is where Mozambique runs lightest.

Software development accounts for 9.68% of sampled conversations — slightly under the global 11.51%, but nearly one in ten conversations is matched to building software. That is a real, present demand for engineering depth, and it is exactly the gap I can help close. If you are turning one of those conversations into something that has to survive contact with reality — shipping an app to the App Store or Google Play, wiring up AI agents or MCP tooling, building a backend with auth that holds up, mobile development, or the reliability engineering that keeps a service standing after launch day — that is the work I do, and I do it informally and for free. The conversation share is already there. When yours becomes a build, I am one message away.
