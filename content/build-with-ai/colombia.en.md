In Colombia, 19.22% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Colombia

The number that should rearrange your assumptions is 71.5%. That is how much larger Colombia's share of Education & Learning conversations is than Korea's — 19.22% against 11.21% — and Korea is the country with an Anthropic Usage Index of 3.78, more than triple Colombia's 1.18. Colombia also runs ahead of Korea on conversations matched to software development tasks, 11.8% versus 10.42%, a 13.2% edge. Whoever expected usage patterns to simply track national income will not find that ordering here.

The learning signal goes deeper than one category. Conversations that looked like coursework account for 30.08% of Colombian usage, close to double the global 16.45%. A reasonable reading — and it is a reading, not a certainty — is that people are using these tools as instruments for acquiring and practising skills, with code as a major part of the material. The composition supports it: after Content Creation & Copywriting at 27.15% (global 22.72%), education and software are the next two topics, both above their global shares.

Equally telling is what is missing. Research & Intelligence sits at 6.56%, a full 40% below the global 10.94%. Meanwhile Document Processing & Extraction runs 41.9% above average (6.13% versus 4.32%), and conversations matched to office and administrative support tasks are 22.4% above average. The Colombian pattern is weighted toward producing and processing things — text, documents, code — rather than open-ended investigation. And the work-versus-study ledger is nearly even: 45.4% of conversations are work-related against 30.08% coursework, with automation and augmentation split almost exactly in half at 50.02% to 49.98%.

None of this describes who the users are, only what the conversations looked like, and a single snapshot cannot say where any of it is heading. What it does say is that Colombian usage already tilts toward learning-by-building and getting concrete output out the door.
