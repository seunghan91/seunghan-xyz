In Burkina Faso, 28.4% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Burkina Faso

The number that upends any tidy rich-country/poor-country ranking is this one: 28.4% of sampled conversations from Burkina Faso are about education and learning — more than double the global 13.23%, and 153% higher than Korea's 11.21%. Korea's usage index dwarfs Burkina Faso's (3.78 against 0.15, rank 113 of 121), yet on this topic the smaller number leads the larger one outright. Volume and intensity are different things, and the conversations from Burkina Faso are intensely concentrated on learning.

The same signature repeats from another angle: 38.76% of conversations look like coursework or learning, against a global 16.45%, while only 30.98% are work-related versus 43.36% globally. Conversations matched to "Educational Instruction and Library" job tasks run at 19.19%, half again the global 12.79%, and document processing and extraction shows up in 7.6% of conversations — about 76% above the global 4.32%. A careful reading, hedged as a guess, is that these tools are being used as serious study and document machinery: working through material, restructuring text, pulling content out of documents. What the data cannot tell us is who is on the other side of the screen. Coursework-shaped conversations are not evidence of students; they are evidence of conversations that resemble coursework, nothing more.

There is also a quieter fact worth stating plainly. Conversations matched to software development tasks make up 10.92% of Burkina Faso's sample — 4.8% higher than Korea's 10.42%. The lead is narrow, but it exists, in the country with the far lower usage index. Meanwhile the automation-leaning share sits at 52.6% against 47.4% augmentation, slightly more delegate-the-whole-task than the global 48.62%, and research and intelligence topics come in about 40% below the global average. Content creation holds the second-largest topic share at 25.92%. Taken together, the picture is of conversations aimed at learning, writing, and building working things — with sales-related tasks notably underrepresented at 6.57% versus 9.14% globally.
