## What the data says about Belgium

The most interesting number in Belgium's data is not how much the country uses these tools, but what the conversations are for. Belgium's Anthropic Usage Index sits at 3.8, ranking 13th out of 121 countries — marginally ahead of Korea at 3.78. So usage intensity is roughly on par with a country famous for engineering output. Yet only 32.84% of Belgian conversations are work-related, against a global average of 43.36%. Meanwhile 28.26% look like coursework or learning, compared with 16.45% globally. The country is using these tools at full intensity, but tilting hard toward understanding things rather than delegating tasks.

That tilt shows up everywhere in the detail. Education and learning topics account for 18.63% of sampled conversations, versus 13.23% globally — a 40.8% gap, the largest positive deviation in Belgium's profile. The same pattern appears in conversations matched to job tasks, where "educational instruction and library" tasks run 15.84% against 12.79% globally. And while automation-leaning conversations are 42.7% against 57.3% augmentation — the global split is nearly reversed at 48.62% — suggesting Belgian conversations more often treat the tool as a collaborator than as a substitute. Conversations matched to software development tasks, by contrast, come in at 8.65%, well under the global 11.51%.

Compared with Korea specifically, Belgium leads education and learning conversations by 66.2% (18.63% versus 11.21%) and hobbies and lifestyle by 62.7% (10.48% versus 6.44%). Content creation and copywriting, the top topic almost everywhere, sits at 21.94%, essentially at the global average of 22.72%.

A plausible reading, and it is only a reading, is that Belgium's position — a small, multilingual country sitting at the centre of a much larger European economy — makes depth a rational strategy. When your market, your collaborators, and your institutions all speak different languages, the value of using an assistant to learn faster may simply outweigh the value of using it to produce more volume. Whether or not that explains the numbers, the numbers themselves are unambiguous: this is not a country waiting for someone else's playbook.

If you are reading this from Belgium, that learning-heavy profile likely resembles the conversations you already have.
