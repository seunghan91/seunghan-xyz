## What the data says about Pakistan

The number that breaks the expected ordering is 14.96%. That is the share of sampled conversations in Pakistan matched to software development tasks — higher than the global average of 11.51%, and higher than Korea's 10.42%. By raw usage volume the two countries sit far apart: Pakistan's Anthropic Usage Index is 0.31, ranked 101 of 121, against Korea's 3.78. But inside the conversations themselves, the mix in Pakistan leans more toward code than the mix in one of the most saturated AI markets measured. Volume and intensity are different things, and this data only claims the first.

The strongest signal in the whole table is educational weight. Conversations that looked like coursework or learning account for 29.74% of the sample, against a global 16.45% — the largest gap in the dataset. The topic breakdown agrees: Education & Learning takes 22.96% of requests, 73.5% above the global average and more than double Korea's 11.21%, and conversations matched to Educational Instruction and Library job tasks run at 16.17% versus 12.79% globally. A plausible reading is that many sessions blend learning and building in one thread — working through a concept and immediately applying it — though the data shows the pattern, not the motive.

Content Creation & Copywriting is the single biggest topic at 28.75%, above the global 22.72% and a shade above Korea's 27.3%. The automation split sits near balance: 50.69% of conversations lean toward handing tasks off, 49.31% toward collaboration, slightly more directive than the global 48.62%. The one clear underweight is Research & Intelligence at 6.95%, about 36% below the global average — in this snapshot, the conversations skew toward making things rather than investigating them. Work conversations at 40.32% run a little under the global 43.36%, consistent with how much of the sample reads as study-shaped.

So the profile is concrete: learning-dense, code-forward, writing-heavy, with Pakistan ahead of Korea on all three of those topic shares.
