## What the data says about Kuwait

The number that should stop you is not Kuwait's overall usage index. At 0.92, Kuwait ranks 65 of 121 countries, while Korea sits at 3.78 — and yet on three of the five most common conversation topics, Kuwait's share of usage runs ahead of Korea's. The clearest case is hobbies and lifestyle: 11.99% of sampled conversations matched to Kuwait, against just 6.44% in Korea, an 86.2% higher share. Education and learning follows at 15.71% versus Korea's 11.21% (40.1% higher), and even software development edges ahead, 11.35% to 10.42%. A simple ordering that puts a higher-index country "ahead" everywhere does not survive contact with this data.

What that plausibly reflects is a particular texture of use. Only 40.06% of Kuwait's conversations are work-related, below the global 43.36%, while 15.11% look like coursework or learning. Read alongside the elevated hobbies and education shares, the picture is of tools woven into life outside the office — side projects, study, personal experiments — at least as much as into formal work tasks. That is an interpretation, not a certainty; the data describes what conversations looked like, not who was having them. But it is consistent with a usage mix that is broader than a purely professional one.

The automation-versus-augmentation split is nearly identical to the global pattern: 48.86% of conversations lean toward handing tasks over to the model, 51.14% toward collaboration, against a global 48.62%. One place Kuwait diverges downward is content creation and copywriting, at 18.9% versus a global 22.72%, and conversations matched to arts, design, entertainment, sports, and media job tasks run 17.5% below the global share. Less of the usage is shaped like a content mill; more of it looks like learning, building, and research, with research and intelligence at 11.47%, slightly above the global 10.94%.
