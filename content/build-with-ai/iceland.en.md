In Iceland, 13.9% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about Iceland

Iceland posts an Anthropic Usage Index of 4.02, ranked 8th out of 121 countries — ahead of Korea at 3.78. For a small island in the North Atlantic, that is the number that breaks the simple ordering most people carry in their heads: usage intensity here is not a function of market size or proximity to any tech hub. Per capita, conversations from Iceland sit in the top tier of everywhere the data covers, and they outpace a country, Korea, that most observers would reflexively place near the front.

The composition of those conversations is where Iceland's profile gets genuinely unusual. Only 36.46% of conversations matched to job tasks are work conversations, against a global 43.36% — a noticeably leisure-weighted split. The standout category is Hobbies & Lifestyle at 13.9% of conversations: 46.5% above the global 9.49%, and 115.8% higher than Korea's 6.44%. The data can't say why, but one plausible reading is that in a small, tightly connected society, these tools get pulled into personal projects and side pursuits just as naturally as into paid work. Meanwhile the categories that dominate globally run quiet here: Content Creation & Copywriting sits at 16.4% versus a global 22.72%, and conversations matched to arts, design, entertainment, sports, and media tasks come in at 11.3% against 13.55%. Education-shaped conversations are 9.9% versus a global 13.23%, with coursework-style conversations at 14.6% against 16.45% — so the non-work share isn't flowing through study-shaped requests either.

Where the building signal does appear, it is solid. Conversations matched to software development tasks make up 10.6% — fractionally ahead of Korea's 10.42% — and Research & Intelligence runs at 13.3% against a global 10.94%, the second place where Iceland clearly overshoots. The automation-versus-augmentation balance is nearly even, 48.38% to 51.62%, almost exactly the global line, so the style of use isn't skewed toward handing work off wholesale. Taken together, the snapshot suggests people here reach for these tools to investigate, tinker, and build on their own terms more than to mass-produce content.
