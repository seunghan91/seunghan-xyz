## What the data says about Georgia

Georgia's overall usage index is 1.8, putting it 36th of 121 countries — about half of Korea's 3.78. But look past the volume and the ordering flips in a way that defies the usual assumptions. Conversations matched to software development tasks make up 15.24% of Georgia's sampled traffic, against a global average of 11.51% and Korea's 10.42%. Georgia uses these tools less than Korea in aggregate, yet a substantially larger share of what Georgia asks about is code: 46.3% higher than Korea on exactly the dimension a simple ranking would predict Korea to dominate.

The rest of the profile sharpens that picture. The single biggest category worldwide, content creation and copywriting at 22.72%, accounts for only 18.25% in Georgia — the country's largest shortfall against the global average. Conversations matched to office and administrative support tasks run at 5.33%, a third below the global 7.89%. Meanwhile research and intelligence comes in at 11.96%, above its global 10.94%. The plausible reading, hedged as any reading of one snapshot must be, is that Georgia's conversations cluster around making and investigating things rather than producing routine text around them. The automation-versus-augmentation split, 48.09% against 51.91%, sits almost exactly on the global 48.62%, so this is not a story of handing work over wholesale — it is a story about which work shows up.

Georgia also leads Korea in two quieter corners. Hobbies and lifestyle conversations are 10.81% of the sample, 67.9% higher than Korea's 6.44% and above the global 9.49% — the largest Korea-facing gap in the data. Education and learning runs at 12.62% versus Korea's 11.21%, even though the coursework share of 12.29% sits well under the global 16.45%. Conversations here look less like assignments and more like self-directed learning mixed with personal projects. Taken together with the work share of 41.99%, near the global 43.36%, the pattern is of tools woven into both work and off-hours building.

That is precisely where Seunghan's offer applies. Shipping an app through store review, connecting an AI agent to real systems with MCP tooling, getting backend and auth right before real users arrive, mobile development, and reliability engineering that keeps a project alive after launch — these are the problems that tend to follow a profile like Georgia's. The time gap between Seoul and Georgia suits asynchronous back-and-forth, and the help is free and informal. If you are already building something, that is the whole qualification.
