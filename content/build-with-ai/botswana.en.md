## What the data says about Botswana

The number that should rearrange your assumptions is this one: 27.68% of sampled conversations from Botswana are about education and learning — more than double the global share of 13.23%, and 146.9% higher than Korea's 11.21%. On the Anthropic Usage Index, Botswana sits at 0.52 against Korea's 3.78, which a lazy reading would file under a simple rich-country ordering. The topic mix refuses that reading. Overall volume may be lower, but where the conversations point is striking: coursework-style exchanges make up 37.33% of traffic here, against a global 16.45%, while work conversations account for 35.48% versus 43.36% globally. This is a usage profile tilted toward learning how to build, not merely delegating tasks to a tool.

A plausible reading — and it is a reading, since these figures describe what conversations looked like, not who had them — is that people in Botswana are reaching for AI as a study partner and tutor as much as a productivity shortcut. The job-task matching backs that up: conversations matched to educational instruction and library tasks run at 16.73%, about 31% above the global share, and office and administrative support tasks also over-index at 9.72% versus 7.89%. Meanwhile the automation-versus-augmentation split is nearly even, 51.52% to 48.48%, slightly more automation-leaning than the global 48.62%. People here are not only asking for answers; a substantial share of the time they are working alongside the model on the task itself.

Just as notable is what is absent. Research and intelligence conversations come in at 5.66%, roughly half the global 10.94%, and hobbies and lifestyle at 6.27% against 9.49%. Software development sits at 9.59%, a bit under the global 11.51%, while content creation and copywriting at 26.94% runs slightly above the global 22.72%. The picture is concentrated: education, writing, and office-flavoured work dominate, with comparatively little of the exploratory or recreational use that pads the averages elsewhere. Whether you are in Gaborone, Francistown, or Maun, that concentration suggests the constraint is not curiosity — it is the next step, turning coursework-shaped knowledge into working software that ships.
