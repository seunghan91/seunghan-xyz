In El Salvador, 19.26% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about El Salvador

Seunghan — the Seoul-based engineer offering free, informal technical help — here's the El Salvador section prose:

El Salvador's usage index is 0.59, ranked 78 of 121 — well under Korea's 3.78. And yet conversations matched to Education & Learning topics make up 19.26% of El Salvador's sample, against Korea's 11.21%. That is a 71.8% higher share from the country with a fraction of the overall usage. If you assumed the ranking predicted the composition, this is where the assumption breaks: lower volume, but a mix tilted far more heavily toward learning.

The coursework signal is the strongest line in the whole dataset. Conversations that looked like coursework or learning account for 27.67% of El Salvador's sample; the global share is 16.45%. One plausible reading — a guess, but a data-grounded one — is that the tool is being used as a study partner: explanations requested, concepts checked, exercises worked through. Whether that reflects classroom work, self-directed curricula, or certification prep, the conversations say people are investing in understanding rather than only in output.

The technical lean is equally concrete. Conversations matched to software development tasks run at 15.24%, versus 11.51% globally and 10.42% in Korea — a 46.3% lead over Korea. Job-task matches in the Computer and Mathematical category reach 28.17% against a global 23.8%. Meanwhile Research & Intelligence sits at just 6.74%, far below the global 10.94%. The pattern that suggests: fewer "go gather this for me" requests, more "help me work through this problem" sessions.

On the ordinary axes, El Salvador looks almost exactly like the world. Work-related conversations are 43.34% against a global 43.36%. Automation-leaning requests edge augmentation 52.38% to 47.62%, close to the global 48.62%. Content Creation & Copywriting tops the topic list at 22.51%, statistically indistinguishable from the global 22.72%. Even Hobbies & Lifestyle, at 8.13%, runs above Korea's 6.44%. Nothing here reads as an edge case — it reads as a peer with a particular emphasis.

That emphasis — learn it, then build it — is precisely where my help is aimed. If you are in El Salvador and your conversations are turning into a real project, I can help with the unglamorous distance between a working prototype and a shipped product: getting apps into the stores, mobile development more broadly, backend and auth that survive real users, AI agent and MCP tooling when you want to build on top of these models rather than only talk to them, and reliability engineering for when something you made starts getting used. The 15.24% says the building is already happening; the offer is for everything after the first run.
