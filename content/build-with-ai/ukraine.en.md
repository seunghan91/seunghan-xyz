In Ukraine, 11% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about Ukraine

A usage index of 0.78, ranked 68th of 121 countries, sounds like a modest number until it is set next to what the conversations themselves contain. Of all sampled conversations from Ukraine, 15.31% were matched to software development tasks — compared with 10.42% in Korea, a country whose usage index of 3.78 is nearly five times higher. So the Ukrainian sample is smaller in volume but denser in code. Whoever expected the richer country's conversations to be the more technical ones would have the ordering backwards.

The tilt away from routine is just as clear on the negative side. Conversations matched to office and administrative support tasks come in at 6.33%, about a fifth below the global 7.89%, and research and intelligence tasks at 9.2%, down from a global 10.94%. Meanwhile 18.77% of conversations looked like coursework or learning, above the global 16.45% — which says the dialogues resembled study, nothing more; the data identifies task types, not the people behind them. And hobbies and lifestyle accounts for 11% of conversations, a striking 70.8% above Korea's 6.44%. One plausible reading, offered as a guess rather than a conclusion, is that these tools are being reached for when there is something specific to make or figure out — a program, a problem set, a side project — rather than for general office throughput.

Nothing here suggests passivity, either. The automation-versus-augmentation split is 48.9% to 51.1%, almost exactly the global balance, which means the conversations are as much about working alongside the tool as handing tasks to it. Content creation and copywriting leads the topic list at 24.66%, slightly above the global 22.72%, but it is the combination beneath it — software development at 15.31%, education and learning at 12.66%, hobbies at 11% — that gives the Ukrainian data its character: a profile weighted toward building and learning, even where overall usage is thinner. The 40.61% share of work-related conversations, a touch below the global 43.36%, points the same direction.

That profile is also a fair description of the problems I spend my own time on, so it is where my offer of help is most concrete. If you are shipping an app and fighting store review, I have done that. The data says the conversations from Ukraine are already about exactly this kind of work. I would be glad to be useful in one of them.
