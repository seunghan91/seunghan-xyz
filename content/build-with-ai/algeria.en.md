In Algeria, 32.55% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Algeria

The number that breaks the expected ordering is this: conversations from Algeria matched to software development tasks make up 13.02% of the sample — above the global average of 11.51%, and above Korea's 10.42%. Korea's Anthropic Usage Index is 3.78, near the top of the table. Algeria's is 0.56, ranked 81 of 121. If raw usage intensity told you where the building happens, the coding share would follow the index. It doesn't. Proportionally, Algeria's conversations are a quarter more about software development than Korea's.

What dominates Algeria's data even more is learning. Nearly half of sampled conversations — 47.12% — read as coursework or study, against a global average of 16.45%. Conversations matched to Education and Learning topics account for 32.55%, where the global figure is 13.23% and Korea's is 11.21% — a 190.4% lead over Korea. The job-task matching agrees: tasks in Educational Instruction and Library work appear at 19.96% versus 12.79% globally. One caveat matters here: these are conversations that look like coursework, not evidence of who is typing them. What the pattern plausibly reflects is heavy use of these tools as study instruments — explaining, drilling, reworking dense material. Document Processing and Extraction running at 7.43% against a global 4.32% fits that reading: turning documents into something usable.

The mirror image is the work share. Only 21.8% of Algeria's conversations are work-related, against 43.36% globally, and Sales and Related task matches run at 6.47% versus 9.14%. This profile tilts toward learning and making rather than office task throughput. Yet the automation-versus-augmentation split — 47.43% to 52.57% — sits almost exactly on the global line. People in Algeria are collaborating with these tools in the same balance as everyone else, not using them differently in kind.

Where this connects to what I can help with: if your conversations already lean toward software development and document processing, the distance between a working prototype and a shipped product is usually tooling, not talent. I help people get apps onto the App Store and Google Play, build AI agents and MCP servers, set up backends and auth that hold up under real users, and make mobile apps reliable.
