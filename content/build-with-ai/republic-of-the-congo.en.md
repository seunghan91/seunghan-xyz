In Republic of the Congo, 25.21% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Republic of the Congo

The most striking number in the Republic of the Congo's data is not the usage index — 0.24, ranking 106 of 121, well below Korea's 3.78 — it is what people do once they are in the conversation. Nearly a third of all conversations, 31.38%, look like coursework or learning, against a global average of 16.45%. And when conversations are matched to job tasks, Education & Learning takes 25.21% of the sample, compared with 13.23% globally — a 90.6% gap, the single most distinctive trait in this country's profile. In a dataset like this, that plausibly reflects people treating the model less as a shortcut and more as a tutor: a place to work through material, test understanding, and build skills one exchange at a time.

Here is the part that breaks any tidy assumption about who leads whom. Conversations matched to education and learning tasks run at 25.21% in the Republic of the Congo versus 11.21% in Korea — 124.9% higher. And conversations matched to software development tasks come in at 12.19% against Korea's 10.42%, a 17% edge. Korea's overall usage intensity is far higher, yet on these two specific slices the Republic of the Congo is ahead. Software development at 12.19% also slightly exceeds the global 11.51%, so building with code is not a niche activity in this sample — it sits right at the centre of how these tools get used.

The rest of the profile hangs together with that picture. Work-related conversations are 33.77% against a global 43.36%, and automation-leaning requests edge out augmentation 53.34% to 46.66%, where globally automation sits at 48.62% — requests here lean a little more toward handing a task over wholesale. Content creation and copywriting leads all topics at 26.52%, above the global 22.72%. Document processing and extraction, at 7.17% versus 4.32% globally (+66%), is the second most distinctive signal, and it lines up with conversations matched to office and administrative support tasks running at 10.16% against a global 7.89% — while hobbies and lifestyle, at 5.73% versus 9.49%, barely registers. Sales-related tasks tell the same story from the other side: 6.57% against a global 9.14%. This is a practical, document-heavy, output-focused pattern of use.
