## What the data says about Kazakhstan

The number that breaks the expected ordering is 17.35%. That is the share of sampled conversations in Kazakhstan matched to education and learning tasks — higher than Korea's 11.21%, higher than the global 13.23%, and the single most distinctive feature of the country's profile at +31.1% against the world average. Korea's overall usage index is 3.78; Kazakhstan's is 0.71, putting it 71st of 121 countries. Yet on the category that most resembles people actively teaching themselves something, the lower-index country runs a share 54.8% above the higher one. A simple rich-country-leads reading does not survive contact with this table.

That pattern repeats. Conversations matched to software development tasks are 11.77% of Kazakhstan's sample versus Korea's 10.42%. Hobbies and lifestyle sits at 9.99% against Korea's 6.44%. Meanwhile the coursework and learning share — 23.06% against a global 16.45% — suggests, and this is a guess from the shape of the data rather than a measurement of intent, that the tools are being used heavily as a way to work through problems and build understanding, not just to get finished output. Work-shaped conversations are 36.37%, below the 43.36% global share, which fits the same reading.

The automation-versus-augmentation split is essentially the global one: 49.26% automation-leaning, 50.74% augmentation-leaning. People are delegating some tasks and collaborating on others in roughly even measure. Content creation and copywriting leads the topic list at 22.53%, almost identical to the global 22.72%. Research and intelligence is the one category that runs light, at 9.29% versus 10.94% globally.

Two cautions before reading too much into any of this. These figures describe what conversations looked like, not who was having them — a coursework-shaped conversation does not mean a student wrote it. And this is one snapshot; nothing here shows direction or speed, only a single cross-section.
