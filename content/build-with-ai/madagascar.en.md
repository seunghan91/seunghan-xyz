In Madagascar, 21.63% of sampled conversations were matched to software development tasks. In Korea that figure is 10.42%.

## What the data says about Madagascar

Madagascar's Anthropic Usage Index sits at 0.1, near the bottom of the 121 countries measured, while Korea's is 3.78. That gap is real, and it mostly reflects how few people in Madagascar have access to these tools at all. But look past the raw index and something more interesting appears: of the conversations that do happen in Madagascar, 21.63% are matched to software development tasks — nearly double the global share of 11.51%, and 107.6% higher than Korea's own 10.42%. Whatever else is true, the Malagasy conversations are unusually engineering-heavy. When people there do open an AI assistant, they are far more likely than the global average to be asking it about code.

The shape of the rest of the usage reinforces this. Education and learning topics account for 17.32% of conversations, well above the global 13.23% and 54.5% higher than Korea's 11.21%. Coursework-like conversations make up 25.59% of the total, compared with 16.45% globally. Taken together with the software share, a plausible reading — and it is only a reading, since these figures describe conversations, not the people having them — is that the usage is concentrated around building things and learning to build things, rather than around sales, research, or general office work. Job-task matches lean "Computer and Mathematical" at 29.23% against a global 23.8%, while "Sales and Related" runs 30.4% under the global average. Research and intelligence topics, at 5.36%, come in at roughly half the global share of 10.94%.

On the work itself, Madagascar splits 52.27% automation-leaning against 47.73% augmentation, slightly more automation-tilted than the global 48.62/51.38 split, and 45.5% of conversations are work-related overall. Content creation and copywriting is the single largest topic at 25.87%, a bit above the global 22.72% — so alongside the coding there is a healthy layer of writing, which fits a country where French and English both serve as bridges to wider markets. Document processing and extraction, at 6.48% versus a global 4.32%, also punches above its weight, the kind of unglamorous task that shows up when someone is shipping something real with paperwork attached.
