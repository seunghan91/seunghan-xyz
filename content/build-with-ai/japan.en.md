## What the data says about Japan

Japan's usage data has an anomaly that cuts against any lazy assumption about how a mature economy uses AI: conversations matched to Business Process & Operations tasks make up 7.74% of the Japanese sample, against a global average of just 4.7%. That is a 64.7% overshoot — the single most distinctive figure in the country's profile. Plausibly, it reflects a dense landscape of established organisations whose daily friction is procedural: approvals, documentation, coordination, the quiet machinery of keeping large operations moving. That is a reading of the data, not a fact about it, but no other topic in the Japanese sample diverges from the global norm anywhere near as sharply.

The surrounding numbers reinforce the picture of work-centred use. 47% of conversations in Japan are work-related, above the global 43.36%, while coursework and learning conversations account for only 5.85% — barely a third of the global 16.45%. Since these figures describe conversations matched to task types rather than the people behind them, the careful conclusion is simply that Japanese usage skews toward getting things done and away from study-shaped requests. Research & Intelligence is also overrepresented, at 12.83% versus 10.94% globally, a 17.3% lead that pairs naturally with the operations finding: find the information, then move the process along.

Content Creation & Copywriting tops the topic list at 22.07%, almost exactly the global 22.72%. Software Development conversations sit at 10.04%, slightly below the global 11.51%, and the automation-augmentation split — 47.45% to 52.55% — tracks the global balance closely. Nothing about the profile suggests heavy delegation or heavy hand-holding; it suggests tools folded into existing routines.

Against Korea, the comparison deserves a plain statement in both directions. Korea's overall Anthropic Usage Index of 3.78 is roughly double Japan's 1.91, which ranks 32nd of 121 countries. But Japan leads Korea outright in Hobbies & Lifestyle conversations: 9.49% versus 6.44%, a 47.4% margin. Japanese usage is, by this measure, the more personal of the two.
