In Slovakia, 12.1% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about Slovakia

Slovakia's Anthropic Usage Index is 1.43, rank 47 out of 121 countries. Korea's is 3.78. A simple ordering would predict that Korea leads on everything underneath those headline figures — and it does not. Conversations matched to software development tasks make up 12.53% of the Slovak sample, compared with Korea's 10.42%. Conversations in the education and learning category sit at 13.24% against Korea's 11.21%. And hobbies and lifestyle conversations reach 12.1%, nearly double Korea's 6.44% and 27.5% above the global average of 9.49%. On the dimensions closest to building and learning, the smaller-volume country is ahead of one of the heaviest AI users measured.

The hobbies figure is the one that would surprise an outside observer most. It is the single most distinctive feature of the Slovak profile, and it pairs with an unusual absence: content creation and copywriting, the biggest category worldwide at 22.72%, accounts for only 17.63% of Slovak conversations, about a fifth below the norm. Conversations matched to arts, design, entertainment, sports, and media tasks are similarly low at 11.27%. So the elevated hobby share is not a surge of marketing-flavoured or media-flavoured usage. Read alongside the software development figure — itself above both the global 11.51% and Korea — it plausibly points toward tinkering: building small things for their own sake, learning by making, using these tools as a workshop rather than a content pipeline. That is an interpretation of the shape of the numbers, and it should be held loosely, but the shape is consistent.

The rest of the profile supports the same reading. Only 37.27% of Slovak conversations are work-related, well under the global 43.36%, while 18.05% look like coursework or learning, above the global 16.45%. Research and intelligence conversations run at 11.35%. The automation-versus-augmentation split is nearly even — 48.3% to 51.7%, essentially the global balance — meaning these conversations hand off whole tasks about as often as they think alongside the person, with no strong tilt either way.
