## What the data says about Malta

Malta's Anthropic Usage Index is 4.0 — ninth out of 121 countries measured, and ahead of South Korea's 3.78. If you assumed AI usage intensity would simply track country size or the usual pecking order of tech hubs, this snapshot disagrees. A small island with English as a working language and an internationally oriented services economy sits near the very top of the table, ahead of the country this page is written from.

The composition of that usage is arguably more telling than the rank. 48.04% of conversations from Malta are work-related, against a global 43.36%. Content Creation & Copywriting dominates at 24.05%, followed by Research & Intelligence at 11.44% and conversations matched to software development tasks at 11.24%. The automation-versus-augmentation split is 49.89% to 50.11% — nearly even, and a touch more automation-leaning than the global 48.62%. One plausible reading, hedged as a guess: in a compact economy where a single project often needs copy, research, and code from the same pair of hands, the tool gets handed complete tasks rather than narrow ones.

The most distinctive feature of Malta's profile is what is missing. Conversations in Education & Learning make up 10.49% of the sample, well under the global 13.23% — a 20.7% gap — and conversations matched to sales-related tasks come in at 7.7% versus 9.14% globally. Taken at face value, the Maltese sample tilts away from practice-shaped exchanges and toward output-shaped ones. That is an inference from conversation labels, nothing more; these figures say nothing about who is behind the keyboard.

Against Korea specifically, Malta leads in two places worth naming. Conversations matched to software development tasks: 11.24% versus Korea's 10.42%. And Hobbies & Lifestyle at 9.4% versus Korea's 6.44% — a 46% gap — which suggests usage there extends comfortably past work obligations into personal projects, experiments, and things built for their own sake.
