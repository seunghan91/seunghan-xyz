In Egypt, 21.5% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Egypt

Egypt's conversations lean slightly toward automation — 50.87% against 49.13% augmentation — edging past the global automation share of 48.62%. That might seem like a small thing to lead with, but consider what it sits next to. A country ranked 98 of 121 on the Anthropic Usage Index (0.36, against Korea's 3.78) is, in raw volume, a light user of these tools. Yet the conversations that do happen are not tentative or assistive. About half of them hand tasks over outright. The pattern reads less like cautious dabbling and more like people arriving with specific work to delegate — though that is an inference from the shape of the data, not a fact about who is typing.

The shape gets sharper in the topic breakdown. Conversations matched to software development tasks account for 19.01% of the sample, 65.2% above the global 11.51%, and Education & Learning leads everything at 21.5% against a global 13.23%. Coursework-looking conversations make up 28.15% of the total, well above the 16.45% global share — which means the conversations resembled coursework, full stop; the data says nothing about students or anyone else. Meanwhile Content Creation & Copywriting runs below the global average (18.75% versus 22.72%), as do Research & Intelligence and Hobbies & Lifestyle. The profile tilts toward building and learning to build.

Here is where the simple ordering breaks entirely. On software development conversations, Egypt's 19.01% runs 82.4% higher than Korea's 10.42%. On Education & Learning, 91.8% higher than Korea's 11.21%. Even Hobbies & Lifestyle edges Korea, 6.49% to 6.44%. And conversations matched to "Computer and Mathematical" job tasks reach 30.53%, against 23.8% globally. By intensity of technical use per conversation, Egypt's sample is more code-saturated than the country Seunghan works in — a fact he finds genuinely useful to know before offering help, because it changes what help is worth offering.

So, practically: if you are building something with AI in Egypt, the gap Seunghan can close is the one between a working conversation and a shipped product. He offers free, informal help with getting apps onto the stores, AI agent and MCP tooling, backend and auth, mobile development, and reliability engineering. No programme, no application process — bring the thing you are stuck on.
