## What the data says about Switzerland

The number that should overturn your assumptions is 5.02. That is Switzerland's Anthropic Usage Index, placing it third out of 121 countries — comfortably ahead of Korea at 3.78. Anyone who imagines AI adoption as a story where a handful of places lead and everyone else follows has it backwards here: on usage intensity, Switzerland is one of the leaders, full stop.

But the interesting part is what all that usage is *for*. Conversations matched to work tasks account for only 38.78% of the Swiss sample, well below the global 43.36%. Conversations matched to coursework or learning sit at 17.3%, slightly above the global 16.45%. And the split between handing tasks over versus working alongside the model tilts toward collaboration: 44.15% of conversations lean automation, 55.85% augmentation, compared with a global automation share of 48.62%. One plausible reading — hedged, because these figures describe conversations matched to job tasks, not the people having them — is that in Switzerland these tools are woven into learning, research, and personal projects rather than being treated purely as office machinery.

The topic breakdown reinforces that. Content creation and copywriting leads at 22.28%, nearly identical to the global 22.72%. Education and learning follows at 13.54%, research and intelligence at 12.17% — both above their global marks of 13.23% and 10.94%. The two genuinely distinctive numbers are these: conversations matched to hobbies and lifestyle tasks reach 11.12%, some 17% above the global 9.49%, while conversations matched to software development tasks come in at 8.73%, roughly 24% *below* the global 11.51%. Heavy usage, light on code. Set against Korea, the gap is stark: Switzerland's hobbies-and-lifestyle share is 72.7% higher than Korea's 6.44%, and its education-and-learning share is 20.8% higher than Korea's 11.21%.
