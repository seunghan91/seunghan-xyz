## What the data says about Dominican Republic

The most surprising number in Dominican Republic's data is not the usage index — it is what the conversations are about. The country sits at 0.67 on the Anthropic Usage Index, ranked 72 of 121, while Korea posts 3.78. Yet conversations matched to software development tasks account for 14.8% of sampled usage there, comfortably above the global 11.51% and 42% higher than Korea's 10.42%. Education and learning tells the same story: 17.04% of conversations versus Korea's 11.21%, a 52% lead, and 28.8% above the global 13.23%. Even hobbies and lifestyle conversations run 33.2% ahead of Korea's share. A lower overall index plainly does not translate into less technical use; the Dominican profile is simply weighted harder toward learning and building than Korea's is.

What the profile moves away from is just as telling. Research and intelligence conversations are the single most distinctive gap: 6.98% against a global 10.94%, a 36% shortfall — the largest divergence in the whole table. Job tasks matched to sales and related work also come in low, 7.45% versus 9.14% globally. Content creation and copywriting, the top category nearly everywhere, lands almost exactly on the global norm at 23.71% versus 22.72%. So the shape of usage in Dominican Republic is not a scaled-down version of the global average; it is a different mix, pulled toward coursework-shaped and code-shaped conversations and away from research and sales work.

The working patterns reinforce that reading. 46.5% of conversations are work-related against a global 43.36%, and 18.37% look like coursework or learning against 16.45% globally. The automation-versus-augmentation split is notable too: 52.1% of conversations lean toward handing the task over entirely, while the global figure tilts the other way at 48.62%. One careful interpretation is that when people in Dominican Republic bring something to these tools, they more often want a finished result than a collaborator in the loop. That is an inference from a single snapshot, not a verdict — but it is consistent with a profile heavy on concrete, end-to-end tasks.
