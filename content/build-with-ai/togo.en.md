In Togo, 20.73% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Togo

The number that breaks the usual rich-country/poor-country ordering is 84.9%. That is how much larger Togo's share of conversations matched to Education & Learning tasks is than Korea's — 20.73% of sampled activity, against Korea's 11.21%. By raw volume the comparison is lopsided in Korea's favour: Togo's Anthropic Usage Index is 0.22, rank 109 of 121 countries, while Korea's is 3.78. By content, the comparison is lopsided the other way. Conversations matched to software development tasks are 17.27% of Togo's activity versus 10.42% in Korea, a 65.7% lead, and even content creation and copywriting — Togo's largest single category at 28.76% — edges Korea's 27.3%.

The same tilt appears against the global average. Education & Learning runs 56.7% above the global 13.23%, software development 50% above the global 11.51%, and conversations that look like coursework account for 26.3% of activity compared with 16.45% worldwide. What runs low is Research & Intelligence: 5.35%, barely half the global 10.94%. One plausible reading — and it is a reading, not a fact — is that these tools are being used in Togo to make things and to learn the craft of making them, rather than to survey or analyse. A conversation that resembles coursework says nothing about who typed it, but the aggregate shape of the work is legible, and it leans toward practice over theory.

The automation split supports that grain. In 52.28% of Togo's conversations the tool is asked to carry out the task rather than assist with it, above the global 48.62%, which fits a pattern of finished output: copy, documents, working code. Document processing and extraction, at 5.35% against a global 4.32%, points the same practical direction. Conversations matched to arts, design, and media tasks also run above the global share — 15.8% versus 13.55% — while sales-related tasks run well below it, 6.39% versus 9.14%. The overall work share, 39.53% of conversations, sits slightly under the global 43.36%; that describes conversation content, not anyone's employment.
