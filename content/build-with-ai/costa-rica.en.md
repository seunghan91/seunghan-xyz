## What the data says about Costa Rica

Costa Rica ranks 42nd of 121 countries on the Anthropic Usage Index, with a score of 1.57 — less than half of Korea's 3.78. Yet on the single most distinctive measure in its data, Costa Rica is ahead of Korea by a wide margin: 17.39% of sampled conversations matched to education and learning tasks, compared with 11.21% in Korea and 13.23% globally. That's 55.1% above the Korean figure. Anyone expecting the country with the lower overall index to show a narrower, more casual range of uses would find the opposite here. The learning-leaning signal appears twice, in fact: separately, 21.21% of conversations matched to coursework or learning tasks, against a global share of 16.45%.

What does that plausibly reflect? One reasonable reading is that these tools are being used in Costa Rica as instruments for understanding — working through material, unpacking problems, drafting explanations — rather than purely as task-completion engines. That's a guess, and it should stay one: the figures describe conversations matched to job tasks, not the people having them, and a single snapshot can't say anything about direction or momentum. The surrounding numbers sketch a consistent texture, though. Software development tasks take a 12.56% share, above both the global 11.51% and Korea's 10.42%. Research and intelligence tasks run well below the global average — 7.99% against 10.94% — while content creation sits near the norm at 21.42%. The balance between handing work over and working alongside the tool is almost exactly the global split: 48.93% automation-leaning, 51.07% augmentation. And 45.48% of conversations matched to work tasks, a couple of points above the worldwide 43.36%.

Taken together, the portrait is of conversations weighted toward making and learning — writing, studying, building software — in a country whose clocks sit almost opposite Seoul's, which turns out to suit asynchronous collaboration well. Questions travel while one side sleeps; answers wait by morning.
