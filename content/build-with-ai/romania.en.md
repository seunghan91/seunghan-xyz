In Romania, 11.71% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about Romania

The single most interesting number in Romania's data has nothing to do with work. Conversations matched to Hobbies & Lifestyle tasks make up 11.71% of the sample — 23.4% above the global average of 9.49%, and a full 81.8% higher than Korea's 6.44%. Anyone assuming usage maps neatly onto national income would not predict this: a large share of what people bring to these tools here is personal projects, curiosity, and building for its own sake. That is an interpretation, of course. The data only describes what the conversations looked like, not who was having them or why. But a hobbies share this far above the global line, paired with a work share of just 38.96% against a global 43.36%, points to usage that is self-directed rather than employer-driven.

The overall Usage Index is 1.37, putting Romania 49th of 121 countries — Korea sits much higher at 3.78, which mostly reflects how heavily Korean conversations concentrate on the tool. Coursework-style conversations account for 18.05% here, above the global 16.45%, and the automation-versus-augmentation split tilts slightly toward augmentation at 52.12%. People are more often working alongside the model than delegating to it.

On topics, Romania runs ahead of Korea in three of the top five. Conversations matched to software development tasks are 12.7% of the sample — above the global 11.51% and 21.9% higher than Korea's 10.42%. Education & Learning comes in at 12.15% versus Korea's 11.21%, an 8.4% lead. Research & Intelligence is 11.83%, also above the global 10.94%. The largest single category, Content Creation & Copywriting at 21.73%, tracks the global 22.72% almost exactly — it is the technical and exploratory categories where this country's profile diverges upward.

That combination — strong software development share, strong research share, an unusually high hobbies share — is the profile of people building things because they want to.
