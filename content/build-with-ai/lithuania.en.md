In Lithuania, 12.27% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about Lithuania

The most surprising number in Lithuania's data is not about work at all. Conversations about hobbies and lifestyle make up 12.27% of the country's sampled total, against a global average of 9.49% — and Korea, whose overall Anthropic Usage Index of 3.78 far exceeds Lithuania's 2.28, records only 6.44% on the same topic. On that one category, Lithuania's share is 90.5% higher than Korea's. A simple ordering by national wealth or overall adoption volume would never predict that.

The shape of the rest of the data reinforces the pattern. Work-related conversations account for 38.72% of Lithuania's total, well below the global 43.36%, while coursework and learning conversations run at 17.48%, a touch above the global 16.45%. Content creation and copywriting leads the topic list at 19.45%, but that is under the global 22.72%. One plausible reading, offered as a guess: with a smaller slice of conversations matched to job tasks, more of the mix is left over for personal and exploratory topics. The figures describe what the conversations looked like, nothing more.

On the technical side, Lithuania tracks the world closely and Korea from above. Conversations matched to software development tasks are 11.38% of the total, essentially level with the global 11.51% and 9.2% higher than Korea's 10.42%. Research and intelligence work takes 12.18%, above the global 10.94%. The automation-versus-augmentation balance — 47.99% against 52.01% — sits almost exactly on the global 48.62% automation figure, suggesting these tools are being folded into workflows in much the same way as elsewhere. Conversations matched to sales-related tasks, at 10.81%, run 18.3% above the global 9.14%, while education and learning topics, at 11.17%, come in 15.6% below the global 13.23%. Lithuania's usage index ranks 28th of 121 countries, and the mix inside that ranking is anything but generic.
