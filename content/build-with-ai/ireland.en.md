## What the data says about Ireland

Conversations matched to software development tasks make up just 7.56% of Ireland's sampled usage, against a global average of 11.51% — a third below par, and the single most counterintuitive figure in the country's data. Yet Ireland is anything but a light user: its Anthropic Usage Index is 3.23, placing it 18th of 121 countries and within sight of Korea's 3.78. Heavy overall use paired with a thin coding share suggests breadth, not absence — these tools woven into many kinds of activity rather than concentrated in engineering.

The rest of the profile supports that reading. Hobbies & Lifestyle takes 13.07% of conversations, 37.7% above the global 9.49% and the country's most distinctive tilt. Research & Intelligence runs at 13.3% versus 10.94% worldwide, Education & Learning at 14.49% versus 13.23%, and conversations matched to 'Educational Instruction and Library' job tasks reach 15.58% against a global 12.79%. Sales and Related tasks over-index too, at 11.11% versus 9.14%. Content Creation & Copywriting leads all topics at 19.33%, though even that sits below the global 22.72% — as if Ireland's usage spreads outward into everything else. These are patterns in conversations, not claims about the people holding them.

The non-work character shows in the totals as well: 36.26% of conversations are work-matched versus 43.36% globally, while the coursework-like share of 15.8% nearly matches the global 16.45%. And on style of use, Ireland leans toward augmentation — 53.26% of conversations look like working alongside the model, against 46.74% automation-leaning, the reverse of the global 48.62% automation share. A reasonable guess is that a population comfortable using these tools across life, not just at a desk, tends to keep a hand on the wheel — but that is a guess.

Where Ireland leads Korea, it leads decisively. Hobbies & Lifestyle conversations run at 13.07% against Korea's 6.44% — roughly double. Education & Learning is 14.49% versus Korea's 11.21%.
