In Jordan, 21.76% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Jordan

Jordan's Anthropic Usage Index is 0.64, placing it 74th of 121 countries — and yet in education and learning conversations it runs 94.1% ahead of South Korea, a country whose index of 3.78 is nearly six times higher. That single comparison breaks any simple ordering by national income: 21.76% of Jordan's sampled conversations match education and learning tasks, against 11.21% in Korea and 13.23% globally. Volume per person and the character of the conversations are clearly different things.

The learning signature shows up twice, from two angles. Beyond the topic share, 30.02% of Jordan's conversations look like coursework or structured learning — nearly double the global 16.45%. One plausible reading, and it is only a reading of conversation patterns rather than of who is typing, is that many sessions treat the model as a tutor and a workbench at once: understand the thing, then make the thing. These figures describe conversations matched to task categories, not anyone's job or enrolment status.

Software development is the second place Jordan leads Korea outright: 15.89% of conversations match software development tasks versus Korea's 10.42%, a 52.5% edge, and +38.1% above the global 11.51%. Hobbies and lifestyle at 7.8% also edges Korea's 6.44%. Meanwhile the sample tilts away from production-of-text work: content creation and copywriting sits at 19.87%, below the global 22.72%, and research and intelligence at 8.61% trails the global 10.94% by about a fifth. Conversations matched to arts, design, entertainment, sports, and media job tasks come in at 11.37% against a global 13.55%. The profile leans toward building and learning rather than drafting and research.

The working style is split almost evenly: 48.93% of conversations lean toward automation, 51.07% toward augmentation, closely tracking the global automation figure of 48.62%. And 37.1% of conversations are work-related, below the global 43.36% — consistent with a sample where learning sits alongside building rather than behind it.
