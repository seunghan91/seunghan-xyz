## What the data says about Bahrain

The number that should reorder your assumptions about Bahrain is 20.17%. That is the share of sampled conversations there matched to education and learning tasks — against a global average of 13.23%, and against 11.21% in Korea, a country whose overall usage index of 3.78 is more than double Bahrain's 1.5. On raw volume Bahrain ranks 44 of 121 countries, but on the texture of the conversations it leads Korea on this measure by nearly 80%. A simple ordering by income or adoption volume would not predict that.

What plausibly sits behind it: 25.11% of Bahraini conversations looked like coursework or learning, compared with 16.45% globally, while work-matched conversations ran at 39.06% against a global 43.36%. One careful reading is that in a small, bilingual island where English circulates widely in daily and commercial life, these tools get used heavily as something to study with and think through — explanation, practice, working material over — alongside their use for output. That is an inference from the shape of the conversations, not a claim about who is having them; the data describes tasks, not people.

The pattern holds elsewhere. Software development tasks account for 11.07% of Bahraini conversations, essentially at the global 11.51% and ahead of Korea's 10.42%. Hobbies and lifestyle sit at 8.31%, above both the global 9.49%... no — above Korea's 6.44%, and just under the global figure. Content creation leads the table at 21.69%, near the global 22.72%. The one clear dip is research and intelligence at 9.04%, about 17% below the global 10.94%. And the automation-versus-augmentation split is nearly even — 47.5% to 52.5% — suggesting conversations that delegate and conversations that collaborate carry almost equal weight.
