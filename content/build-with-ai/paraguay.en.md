In Paraguay, 9.58% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about Paraguay

Paraguay's overall usage index is 0.59, placing it 78th of 121 countries, and Korea's is 3.78 — yet the single most interesting number in this table runs the other direction. Conversations matched to software development tasks account for 12.29% of Paraguay's sampled conversations, against 10.42% in Korea and a global average of 11.51%. A reader assuming that a higher-index country would naturally dominate the technical categories would have that assumption inverted here: on the share of conversation volume that looks like building software, Paraguay leads Korea by about 18%.

That is not the only category where the ordering flips. Education and learning conversations make up 14.7% of Paraguay's sample, roughly 31% above Korea's 11.21%, and hobbies and lifestyle conversations sit at 9.58%, nearly half again Korea's 6.44%. Meanwhile 48.79% of all sampled conversations are work-related, well above the global 43.36%. The composite picture is of conversation volume that leans toward making and learning things rather than toward analysis: research and intelligence, at 8.8%, is the category where Paraguay runs furthest below the global average of 10.94%.

There is also a distinctive tilt in how tasks get handed to the tool. 52.29% of conversations lean toward automation — delegating a task outright — versus 47.71% toward augmentation, while globally automation sits lower at 48.62%. Conversations matched to office and administrative support tasks run at 9.5%, about a fifth above the global 7.89%. A plausible reading is that a comparatively large share of conversations are about getting defined pieces of work executed and finished, though that is a guess from pattern, not something the data states — these figures describe what conversations looked like, not who was having them or why, and this is one snapshot with no history attached to it.

Content creation and copywriting tops the topic list at 22.49%, essentially level with the global 22.72%, so the writing-assistance use case is as present here as anywhere. The differentiators are the software development, education, and hobbies shares — all above Korea's.
