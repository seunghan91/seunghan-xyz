## What the data says about Jamaica

The number that should stop you is 13.53%. That is the share of sampled Jamaican conversations matched to software development tasks — higher than the global 11.51%, and higher than Korea's 10.42%. Korea's overall usage index is 3.78; Jamaica's is 0.66, ranking 73rd of 121 countries. A reader assuming that a lower index means a thinner, less technical mix of usage would be wrong: within Jamaica's conversations, building software takes up a larger slice than it does in one of the most saturated AI markets measured. The volume differs; the character of the use does not follow the same ordering.

The learning signal reinforces this. Education & Learning accounts for 17.38% of Jamaican conversations against 13.23% globally — 31% above average — and 55% above Korea's 11.21%. Conversations that look like coursework make up 18.73%, versus 16.45% worldwide. To be precise about what this means: the conversations resembled learning and study tasks. That says nothing about who is having them, and I won't pretend otherwise. What it does suggest is that a substantial portion of Jamaican usage is oriented toward acquiring skills and then applying them, which fits the software development share sitting right alongside it.

Just as telling is what is missing. Research & Intelligence conversations run at 7.11%, a full 35% below the global 10.94% — the country's single largest divergence from the average in either direction. Meanwhile, conversations matched to Management job tasks come in at 8.39% versus 5.9% globally, a 42% surplus. One plausible reading, and it is only a reading: the usage leans toward producing and directing work rather than open-ended investigation. Content Creation & Copywriting leads the topic list at 25.17%, above the global 22.72%, which points the same way. The automation-versus-augmentation balance is nearly even — 49.49% to 50.51%, close to the global split — and the work share, 43.29%, all but matches the worldwide 43.36%.
