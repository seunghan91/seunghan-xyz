## What the data says about Czechia

The number that should rearrange your assumptions is 12.39%. That is the share of sampled conversations in Czechia matched to Hobbies & Lifestyle tasks — 30.6% above the global average of 9.49%, and 92.4% higher than Korea's 6.44%. Czechia's overall Anthropic Usage Index is 1.84, rank 34 of 121, which is about half of Korea's 3.78. If usage patterns simply scaled with intensity, you'd expect Czechia's profile to look like a quieter copy of Korea's. It doesn't. The single biggest divergence in the whole dataset is Czech conversations drifting toward personal projects and pastimes — people building things because they feel like it.

The engineering side of the data points the same direction. Conversations matched to software development tasks make up 12.29% in Czechia, above the global 11.51% and 17.9% ahead of Korea's 10.42%. Education & Learning conversations also edge Korea, 12.54% to 11.21%. And the category that dominates globally — Content Creation & Copywriting, 22.72% worldwide — sits at just 17.72% in Czechia, a 22.0% deficit that is the country's second-most distinctive trait. One cautious reading: usage here tilts toward making and understanding — code, exploratory learning, hobbies — rather than toward producing text at scale. The data describes what conversations looked like, not who was having them, so that stays a guess.

On everything else, Czechia hugs the global average. Work-related conversations are 39.61% versus 43.36% globally; coursework-flavoured ones are 15.29% versus 16.45%. The automation-versus-augmentation balance — 48.52% against 51.48% — is nearly identical to the worldwide 48.62%. So Czechia doesn't delegate more or less than anyone else; it just brings different topics to the table.

That combination — strong software development share, an outsized hobbyist streak, restrained copywriting demand — is close to my own centre of gravity.
