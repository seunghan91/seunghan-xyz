## What the data says about Sweden

What stands out in Sweden's numbers is not intensity but shape. Sweden's Anthropic Usage Index sits at 2.98, ranked 22 of 121 countries, while Korea's is 3.78. Yet only 38.11% of Swedish conversations matched to job tasks are work-related, well below the global 43.36%. High overall usage with a comparatively light work share suggests these tools have spread past the working day into the rest of life — and the topic breakdown backs that up.

The clearest signal is Hobbies & Lifestyle. It accounts for 11.98% of sampled Swedish conversations, 26.2% above the global average of 9.49% and a striking 86% higher than Korea's 6.44%. Research & Intelligence is the other place Sweden pulls away from the global norm: 12.84% of conversations versus 10.94% worldwide, a 17.4% lead. A plausible reading — and it is a reading, since these figures describe conversations matched to task types, not the people having them — is that Swedish usage leans exploratory: digging into questions and personal projects alongside, or instead of, strictly occupational tasks.

Sweden also leads Korea, modestly, in two categories closer to my own work. Conversations matched to software development tasks make up 11.63% of Sweden's sample against Korea's 10.42%, an 11.6% edge, and conversations that look like education and learning run 11.87% to Korea's 11.21%. Meanwhile Content Creation & Copywriting, the single largest topic at 19.68%, actually trails the global 22.72%. The mix tilts toward building and investigating rather than producing text.

One more number worth sitting with: the automation-versus-augmentation split. Swedish conversations lean toward augmentation, 55.7%, against 44.3% automation-leaning, while globally automation edges ahead at 48.62%. Whatever else that means, it is consistent with people using these tools as collaborators in work they intend to stay involved in, rather than handing tasks over wholesale. Combined with a coursework-and-learning share of 16.93%, slightly above the global 16.45%, the picture is of usage woven into learning and side projects, not just output extraction.
