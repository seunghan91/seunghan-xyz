In Chile, 17.37% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Chile

Start with the number that breaks the usual ranking instinct: conversations matched to education and learning tasks make up 17.37% of Chile's sample — 31% above the global share of 13.23%, and a full 55% above Korea's 11.21%. Chile's overall usage index is 1.84, rank 34 of 121, well under Korea's 3.78, so anyone reading down a league table would expect a thinner, more derivative pattern of use. Instead, Chile leads Korea on three of the five big topics: education, hobbies and lifestyle (8.1% versus 6.44%), and software development tasks (11.63% versus 10.42%). Lower volume, richer mix.

The learning skew is broad, not cosmetic. Coursework-shaped conversations account for 21.73% of everything sampled, against 16.45% globally. At the same time, work conversations run at 47.89% — clearly above the global 43.36% — and the split between handing tasks over and working alongside the model (48.82% automation, 51.18% augmentation) is almost indistinguishable from the global line. A reasonable guess, though only a guess from a single snapshot: people in Chile are using these tools in a studious, hands-on way — treating the model as something to learn with and build with, not just a machine to offload chores onto.

What's scarce is equally informative. Conversations matched to research and intelligence tasks sit at 8.61%, about a fifth below the global 10.94%, and sales-related tasks at 7.67% against 9.14%. Office and administrative support, meanwhile, runs heavy at 9.13% versus 7.89%. Content creation tops the chart at 22.18%, basically the world average. Put it together and the Chilean profile looks like a workshop rather than a consultancy: writing, coursework, code, and paperwork, with relatively little of the analysis-and-persuasion layer that dominates elsewhere.
