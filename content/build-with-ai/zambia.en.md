In Zambia, 28.79% of sampled conversations were matched to education & learning tasks. In Korea that figure is 11.21%.

## What the data says about Zambia

Zambia's overall usage index is 0.15 — rank 113 of 121, against Korea's 3.78 — and yet 10.87% of sampled conversations are matched to software development tasks, a hair above Korea's 10.42% and essentially level with the global 11.51%. That is the number that scrambles any simple rich-country/poor-country reading. Aggregate usage can be low while the conversations that do happen are every bit as technical, proportionally, as those in one of the most AI-saturated countries measured. The data describes conversations, not the people having them, so it cannot say who is behind the keyboard. What it can say is that when these tools get used in Zambia, building software is near the centre of what gets asked.

The bigger weight sits elsewhere: 34.72% of conversations look like coursework or learning, more than double the global 16.45%, and the Education & Learning topic alone accounts for 28.79% of requests against a global 13.23% — and against Korea's 11.21%, a 156.8% lead. Conversations matched to educational instruction and library tasks add a supporting signal at 17.86% versus 12.79% globally. A reasonable, hedged reading is that these tools are being leaned on heavily as study companions and explanation engines, alongside hands-on building.

Work-shaped conversations run at 36.41%, below the global 43.36%, and within them the pattern skews practical. Document processing and extraction takes 6.82% versus a global 4.32%, while research and intelligence sits at 6.18%, well under the global 10.94%. Sales-related tasks are similarly light at 6.42% against 9.14%. Content creation and copywriting, at 24.91%, tracks close to the global 22.72%. The profile that emerges is learn, build, and produce documents — less open-ended analysis, less selling.

There is also a style signal in the 54.12% automation-leaning share versus 45.88% augmentation, against a global automation figure of 48.62%. Conversations in Zambia tilt toward handing the model a task and taking the output, rather than extended back-and-forth collaboration.
