In North Macedonia, 18.4% of sampled conversations were matched to software development tasks. In Korea that figure is 10.42%.

## What the data says about North Macedonia

Korea's Anthropic Usage Index is 3.78. North Macedonia's is 0.98, rank 60 of 121. On raw volume, the gap is wide. But look at what the conversations are actually about, and the ordering flips: 18.4% of conversations in the North Macedonia sample are matched to software development tasks, compared with 10.42% in Korea — a 76.6% lead — and 59.9% above the global average of 11.51%. A reader assuming that heavier usage means more technical usage would get this one wrong. The Macedonian slice of AI conversation is unusually concentrated on code.

That concentration shows up from a second angle as well. Conversations matched to 'Computer and Mathematical' job tasks account for 29.42% of the sample, against 23.8% globally. Meanwhile research and intelligence conversations run at 8.62%, which is 21.2% below the global average — the country's sharpest negative deviation. Read together, the pattern plausibly suggests these tools are being pulled toward building and debugging rather than toward open-ended information gathering. That is an inference from the mix of topics, not a claim about who is typing.

The rest of the profile is close to global norms, which makes the software skew stand out more. Content creation and copywriting leads all topics at 21.33%, nearly identical to the global 22.72%. Education and learning sits at 13.16% versus 13.23% globally, though still ahead of Korea's 11.21%. Hobbies and lifestyle, at 10.93%, clears both the global 9.49% and Korea's 6.44%. Conversations that looked like coursework make up 17.89%, just above the global 16.45% — that describes the shape of the conversations, not the status of the people having them.

On working style, the sample splits almost evenly: 47.13% of conversations lean toward automation, 52.87% toward augmentation, against a global automation share of 48.62%. Work-related conversations are 41.86%, marginally below the global 43.36%. Nothing here reads like tentative use. The balance between handing tasks over and collaborating on them looks like the balance everywhere else — the difference is what the tasks are.

For someone in North Macedonia already deep in that software-heavy 18.4%, Seunghan's offer maps directly onto the hard parts: shipping apps through store review, building AI agent and MCP tooling, backend and auth design, mobile development, and reliability engineering once real users depend on the thing. The data suggests the conversations are already at the building stage; his help is aimed at getting them to the shipped stage.
