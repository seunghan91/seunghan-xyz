In Cambodia, 22.51% of sampled conversations were matched to software development tasks. In Korea that figure is 10.42%.

## What the data says about Cambodia

An index of 0.21 places Cambodia 110th of 121 countries measured, and Korea's 3.78 sits far above it. Yet 22.51% of Cambodia's sampled conversations are matched to software development tasks — nearly twice the global 11.51% and well over twice Korea's 10.42%. By the raw ranking you would expect thin, tentative usage. What the topic breakdown shows instead is usage that is narrow and concentrated: fewer conversations overall, but a much larger share of them spent actually writing and debugging code.

That concentration shows up from several angles. Conversations matched to Computer and Mathematical job tasks make up 32.92% of the total, against 23.8% globally, while Research and Intelligence topics run at 7.04% versus 10.94% globally, and Hobbies and Lifestyle at 6.83% versus 9.49%. Automation-leaning conversations edge out augmentation 52.29% to 47.71%, compared with a global automation share of 48.62%. A careful guess at what this reflects: the people reaching for these tools in Cambodia tend to arrive with a task to finish — something to build, fix, or hand off — rather than to browse or explore. The data cannot say who they are, only what the conversations looked like.

Education is the other place Cambodia runs ahead of Korea, plainly. 16.54% of conversations match education and learning topics versus Korea's 11.21%, and coursework-shaped conversations sit at 19.67% against a global 16.45%. Content creation, at 19.7%, is the second-largest topic, slightly below the global 22.72%. So the overall profile is not a smaller copy of the global average — it is a different mix, tilted toward building software and structured study, lighter on research, sales-flavoured tasks, and lifestyle chat.
