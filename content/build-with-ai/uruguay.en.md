In Uruguay, 9.74% of sampled conversations were matched to hobbies & lifestyle tasks. In Korea that figure is 6.44%.

## What the data says about Uruguay

If you sorted countries by overall AI usage, Uruguay would sit at rank 33 of 121, with an Anthropic Usage Index of 1.89 — half of Korea's 3.78. But sort by what the conversations are actually about, and the ordering flips. Conversations matched to software development tasks account for 13.39% of Uruguay's sample, 28.5% higher than Korea's 10.42%. Education and learning conversations run 15.44% against Korea's 11.21%, a 37.7% larger share. And hobbies and lifestyle — the most lopsided gap in the whole dataset — is 9.74% in Uruguay versus 6.44% in Korea, 51.2% higher. A country with half the usage intensity devotes a bigger fraction of it to code, learning, and personal projects.

The comparison against the global average tells the same story from the other side. Uruguay's software development share is +16.3% above the global 11.51%, and education and learning is +16.7% above the global 13.23%. The country's most distinctive number, though, is a negative one: research and intelligence conversations are 8.73% of the sample, 20.2% below the global 10.94%. Content creation and copywriting leads the topic list at 20.69%, but even that trails the global 22.72%. The profile that emerges is of usage weighted toward building and studying rather than drafting and desk research — an inference from task labels, and worth holding loosely, because these figures describe conversations, not the people having them.

The rest of the numbers are quietly ordinary, which is its own finding. Work-matched conversations are 46.76% of the total, somewhat above the global 43.36%. Coursework-looking conversations are 17.11% versus a global 16.45%. And the automation-versus-augmentation split — 48.01% against 51.99% — sits almost exactly on the global 48.62%. Nothing here suggests an unusual relationship with the tools themselves; Uruguay's distinctiveness is in the topics, not the posture.

That topic mix is the reason this page exists.
